# How can image generation produce distinct creative directions from one brief?

## Guidance

**Explore different visual ideas in parallel, then refine the strongest usable one.** Track novelty relative to a stated comparison set, fit to the brief, and diversity across the candidates. These answer different questions. Several attractive images can share one idea, and an unusual candidate can be unusable. Rendered polish can influence which rough idea gets chosen. [Runco and Jaeger on originality and effectiveness](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400419.2012.650092) · [Amabile on independent assessments](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.43.5.997) · [Design Council Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation)

1. **Keep the core short.** State what the viewer should understand or feel, who will see the image, and the actual constraints. Keep must-have subjects, action, wording, framing, or an established character as applicable. Leave creative decisions open where the user delegated them. Before looking at polished generations, independently write or sketch a few possible readings. The existing OneStep workflow already separates hard requirements and checks the first reading. [Image skill](../../skills/vegabond-image/SKILL.md) · [Visual direction examples](../../skills/vegabond-image/references/style-and-review.md#direct-and-test-the-reading) · [Disney's thumbnail process](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/story/)
2. **Branch on a decision the viewer can see.** Choose an axis that matters to the idea, such as the depicted moment and its evidence, whose viewpoint the viewer occupies, how scale changes the relationship, or how the medium carries meaning. Give each candidate its own concrete interpretation. For deeper changes, combine a principle from another domain, explore more of the existing visual rules, or change one fictional rule and show its consequence. Use the part of a reference that serves the decision, such as a torn-paper edge, spatial rhythm, or mechanism. A reference with a separate job can provide identity or layout. [Boden's creativity framework](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702%2898%2900055-1) · [Finke, Ward and Smith's *Creative Cognition*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262560962/creative-cognition/) · [Chan et al. on analogies](https://joelchan.me/assets/pdf/2011-jmd-distancecommonness.pdf) · [Yilmaz et al. on design heuristics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0142694X16300254)
3. **Make independent rough candidates.** Three directions is a practical starting point for a small exploratory brief. Keep their working scale and polish comparable. Use separate prompts or thumbnails and bring them together for review before extending one branch. If the first round exposes a repeated default, name it and ask for a viable alternative with a visible difference. For example, replace a recurring centered character with a low viewpoint through the consequence of its action. Ensure the replacement still supports the brief. Keep an independent direction available when the easiest candidate to polish dominates. [Dow et al. on parallel prototyping](https://doi.org/10.1145/1879831.1879836) · [Promptify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09337) · [POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13392)
4. **Choose by viewing the work.** Inspect each image without its concept description and give its visible idea a short label. Group candidates with the same mechanism, whether its palette or style varies. Compare surviving directions for surprise and thematic fit, then check mandatory details and final use. Carry the selected branch's composition and identifying cues into the next generation or edit. Compare accepted art with the rough candidate so cleanup preserves the feature that made it worth choosing. [Opal for editorial illustration](https://doi.org/10.1145/3526113.3545621) · [Sketchar for character prototyping](https://doi.org/10.1145/3677102) · [Adobe's product art-direction walkthrough](https://www.adobe.com/learn/photoshop/web/generate-product-backgrounds-firefly-boards)

### Three ways to branch from the same brief

**Example brief.** A text-free editorial picture about a library that lends memories. Each direction shows one reader and the borrowed object. Use the same square crop and the same ink-and-wash medium. The scene should feel intimate and bittersweet. These are concept proposals for comparison.

| Direction | What the viewer actually sees | Risk to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show a decision | At the return slot, a reader's thumb stops a library card from sliding in. A small ink wash of the childhood kitchen appears on the card. | The card and slot must read before the memory is seen. The paused hand must convey reluctance. |
| Show what the memory leaves behind | On the reading table the book holds down the corner of an old tablecloth. A ring from one teacup is visible, and the seated reader rests a hand on the cloth. | The book, cloth and trace must imply one shared event. Otherwise it can read as a quiet still life. |
| Change one rule of the world | A reader opens a pocket book. Its fold opens into a life-size hallway, cropped close enough for one inherited door scratch and the reader's hand to dominate. | The book must still be identifiable as the borrowed object. The spatial break should feel like entering a memory. |

## Applicability and exceptions

| What has gone repetitive | Intervention | Where evidence or control ends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concepts or actions repeat | Change the depicted event, the relationship, or the visual mechanism. Give each prompt an observable difference. A rough from a different medium or a far analogy can contribute one useful relation, such as the tension of a bridge cable or how paper folds conceal an object. | Test the analogy against the brief. Combining unusual nouns can introduce detail while leaving the same composition and story. [Design-by-analogy experiment](https://joelchan.me/assets/pdf/2011-jmd-distancecommonness.pdf) · [Early prompt experiments by Liu and Chilton](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/LiuPromptsAIGenArt_CHI2022.pdf) |
| The same polished style returns | Specify the physical mark, light, scale or surface treatment, for example dry pigment catching rough paper, and test a distinct alternative on the same scene. Use style references by feature and introduce them after the concept is clear when they pull unrelated content along. | Prompt-optimization studies have found repetitive suffixes when one reward is optimized. Art-directed style exploration can keep expressive concepts constant. [PAG, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yun_Learning_to_Sample_Effective_and_Diverse_Prompts_for_Text-to-Image_Generation_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) · [Adobe sketch-to-finish example](https://www.adobe.com/learn/firefly/web/firefly-boards-to-photoshop-workflow) |
| Prompts differ but camera or shape stays similar | Use separate thumbnails, silhouette or layout references. For an established mascot, give identity to the approved model and vary its action or context. Use a crop or edit only when it retains the intended visual relationship. | T-DIG's authors report that their prompt-level method could not consistently vary camera and framing. Spatial control depends on the chosen generator. [T-DIG project and limitations](https://image-study.org/t-dig/) · [ControlNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543) · [Stationery identity guidance](../../skills/vegabond-image/references/stationery-characters.md) |
| A good concept has several plausible executions | Where supported, try a few seeds per direction. Hold the seed and other settings fixed for a small prompt comparison, then rerun the promising direction on other seeds. Midjourney's `--chaos` affects between-result variety, `--stylize` allows more interpretation, and permutations submit separate prompt variants. Diffusers Stable Diffusion exposes guidance and a random generator. | Higher variety or stronger stylistic interpretation can reduce prompt fidelity. Classifier-free guidance has a quality or fidelity versus coverage trade-off, and a fixed seed does not guarantee reproducibility across hardware and models. Codex's built-in `image_gen` in this workflow exposes prompt, references and transparency, with no `seed`, Midjourney settings or CFG argument. Use independent prompts and record repeats on that path. [Ho and Salimans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598) · [Midjourney Chaos](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32099348346765-Chaos-Variety) · [Stylize](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32196176868109-Stylize) · [Permutations](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32761322355597-Permutations) · [Diffusers API](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/api/pipelines/stable_diffusion/text2img) · [Diffusers reproducibility](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/using-diffusers/reproducibility) |

Apply the full exploration cycle when the user values choice, the idea is open, or repeated images are an observed defect. For an established design or a tightly specified edit, preserve the approved geometry, palette or style and vary only a delegated dimension. Illustrators in an interview study used image generation for different jobs, including exploration, automation and communication. Disney's visual-development examples and Adobe's teaching material illustrate workflows. They do not measure the causal effect of a tool. [Ko et al., artist interviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08477) · [Disney visual development](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/visual-development/) · [Seo and Park on designer intervention](https://kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003217124)

## Checks

1. **Separate passing the brief from having a strong idea.** Record mandatory pass, fail or unknown per image. Among eligible images, rate fit and novelty independently. For a small comparison, use three anchored novelty judgments: familiar treatment, new treatment of a familiar idea, and a distinct visual idea relevant to the brief. Keep taste or polish in a separate note. State the comparison set, such as the ordinary baseline and sibling designs. This gives a task-relative judgment of novelty. [Brief acceptance checks](../../skills/vegabond-image/SKILL.md#generate-and-inspect) · [HEIM evaluation dimensions](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/dd83eada2c3c74db3c74db3c7fe1c087513756-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
2. **Measure diversity in the set.** Have reviewers describe the first reading before seeing the prompt or route, tag what was varied, and group ideas that have the same central action, relation or visual mechanism. Then reveal the brief for fit and constraint scoring. Count distinct viable concept families, repeated defaults and the number of generations or edits needed for a usable candidate. Record disagreements. A distance in an image embedding can flag near duplicates for review. A large distance can also come from an irrelevant color, background, or broken geometry. [T-DIG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29003) · [DreamBench++'s comparison with human judgments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16855) · [Wang et al., creativity evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.26/)
3. **Repeat the OneStep comparison when the brief changes materially.** Compare three paths on open briefs: repeat one prompt, vary rendering while holding the concept fixed, and branch the moment, relationship or composition with parallel roughs. The initial trial used three candidates per path, two repeats and three briefs, for 54 first images, and a separate tight edit as a regression check. Match the generator, hard requirements, reference access and candidate count. Test sketches in a separate structural-control comparison. Record available seeds or vary call order where seeds are hidden. Use at least two reviewers and blind the route. Keep concept families among eligible images as the primary set measure, with best eligible novelty and first-pass mandatory pass rate as separate outcomes. Report counts by brief and repeat. Measure work time separately when efficiency matters. Add human ranking before claiming audience preference. ## Evidence

### Findings and how far they transfer

| Finding | Boundary and resulting decision |
| --- | --- |
| **Exposure to examples can narrow ideation.** Jansson and Smith measured recurrence of example features. Wadinambiarachchi et al. randomized 60 participants in a chatbot-avatar sketching task. AI image inspiration increased fixation on the initial example and lowered measured fluency, variety and originality relative to their other conditions. | The latter tested designers' sketches after exposure, with novice participants and a particular task and tool. Try independent roughs before viewing one polished suggestion. Do not use this result to estimate current model output diversity. [Jansson and Smith](https://doi.org/10.1016/0142-694X%2891%2990003-F) · [CHI 2024 experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164) |
| **The timing and kind of assistance matter.** Two experiments reported in *Information Systems Research* found higher rated novelty, relevance and complexity when designers used GenAI during ideation. In the second, with 120 professional designers, ideation also took longer. A separate study with 84 environmental-design students reported benefits of abstract imagery and text-to-image tools under its experimental conditions. | The studies vary in task, participants, tools, stimulus and outcome. They support testing stage and stimulus choices. A polished avatar, an abstract shape reference, and a scaffolded design task expose people to different decisions. [Two design work experiments](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2024.0937) · [Abstract vs. specific stimuli study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060425100061) |
| **Higher novelty can coexist with lower diversity elsewhere.** Zhou and Lee's art-platform analysis found higher peak content novelty and declining mean content novelty. Fu et al. found GenAI-assisted designs rated more unconventional and creative, with no statistically significant perceived-usefulness improvement in their study. Doshi and Hauser found a related individual-versus-collective trade-off in short fiction. | Platform data are observational, and short story evidence is indirect for images. Context matters, including how an evaluation operationalizes novelty. Track distribution and usability along with the best image. [Zhou and Lee](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae052) · [Fu et al., CHI 2026](https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/chi-26.01.pdf) · [Doshi and Hauser](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) |
| **An axis can make variation more purposeful.** POET, tested with 28 users in four task domains, reported greater perceived diversity and fewer prompts to satisfaction. The October 2026 T-DIG preprint varies along a user-selected axis using trained prompt/edit policies and reports gains in its own tests. | POET and T-DIG are particular systems. Handwritten prompt branching is a practical adaptation to test locally. T-DIG's trained policy, metrics and reported effect sizes do not carry over to Codex. [POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13392) · [T-DIG preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29003) |
| **Automated iteration can converge.** A *Patterns* study found that 700 autonomous SDXL–LLaVA caption/regenerate trajectories converged toward 12 dominant motifs despite diverse starting points. PAG identified repeated prompt suffixes in a reward-maximizing baseline. | The loops tested used specific models and conditions. Keep the original brief in each branch, preserve distinct concepts, and use feedback about observable features to guide revision. [Autonomous language-image loops](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2025.101451) · [PAG](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yun_Learning_to_Sample_Effective_and_Diverse_Prompts_for_Text-to-Image_Generation_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) |

### OneStep observations

The 54-image comparison found 19 eligible concept-family counts for branching, compared with 16 for prompt repetition and 10 for rendering variation, summed across two readers and six candidate sets. Branching improved variety for the mascot and coastal market. It failed the personal-memory cue in all six editorial candidates and received only 19/36 first-pass judgments overall, compared with 32/36 for prompt repetition. In a separate edit, both AI reviewers read the hallway as the adult reader's childhood after seeing a distinctive bracelet repeated on the two wrists. Keep branch-specific visual evidence for whatever must remain legible. Scope the result to these briefs, judges and complete prompting workflows. [Trial and failures](../../output/vegabond-image-comparison/creative-exploration/results.md) · [Skill reference](../../skills/vegabond-image/references/creative-exploration.md)

In the local expressive trial, changing the reopening cue from routine bicycle repair to a rising shutter improved the first result for that brief. Across the eight-brief trial, final mandatory-check scores were 127/128 and 126/128 for the compared versions. The trial measured fidelity, with AI reviewers, and did not estimate novelty or human preference. The book-cart trial showed another boundary: the character's identifying marks could survive while all three proposed thank-you images were first read as offering or holding. These cases justify a separate first-reading check and a controlled trial of concept diversity before adopting new defaults.

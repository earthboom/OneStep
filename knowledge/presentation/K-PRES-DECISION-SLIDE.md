# Choose what to keep on a decision slide

## Guidance

Name the audience and the decision it faces. Write the claim the available evidence can support. Show the comparison that makes it interpretable, with relevant units, period and population. Keep any condition that could reverse the decision beside it. Put secondary methods or detail in notes or an appendix the audience can access.

## Applicability and exceptions

A live audience must be able to follow the argument while listening. A standalone handout may need more explanation in the artifact. An export and viewing-size check can establish that a label is visible under those conditions. To claim the audience understood the decision, observe representative readers.

## Checks

For a purchase decision, show the recommended option, its like-for-like comparison and the condition that would change the choice. Render at the intended viewing size. If a necessary qualification is unreadable, simplify or split the comparison before shrinking the text. Verify quantitative claims against their primary source.

## Evidence

Michael Alley's assertion-evidence approach pairs a concise message with visual support in technical talks. W3C WAI's guidance covers limited slide text and legibility at distance. Applying these to a procurement decision and choosing what belongs in backup material are editorial judgments.

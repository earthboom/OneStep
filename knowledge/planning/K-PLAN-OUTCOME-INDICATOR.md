# Choose an outcome indicator for a proposal

## Guidance

Identify who should experience what change. Define the indicator, unit or denominator, observation window, data source and collection method. Record the starting value and when it was measured, then the intended target and by when. Label an unapproved target as proposed. If there is no baseline, specify how and when to obtain it.

## Applicability and exceptions

Delivery counts answer questions about reach or implementation. A claim that beneficiaries improved requires an observation of the intended change. Comparing outcomes before and after an intervention can show a difference. Attributing that difference to the intervention requires further evidence.

## Checks

For a workshop intended to improve independent task completion, keep attendance as a reach measure. Compare the share of eligible participants who complete the same task unaided before training and at follow-up. Record exclusions or missing follow-up observations, the assessment source and the decision threshold. Leave unknown values open.

## Evidence

CDC's evaluation framework explains how to align the question, indicator, measure and source. USAID's 2023 guidance describes how to document a baseline and a time-bound target.

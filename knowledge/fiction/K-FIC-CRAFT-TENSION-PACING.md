# Modulate meaningful anticipation separately from narrative speed

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

Writing Excuses discusses dialogue charged by surrounding stakes [S07] and tools for controlling narrative duration [S08]. Lehne and Koelsch propose a model connecting tension to anticipation of emotionally significant outcomes under instability or uncertainty [S20]. The paper is theoretical synthesis; it does not validate a formula for a specific novel or reader.

### Applied procedure

1. Specify the hoped-for and feared outcomes and why they matter to this character or reader.
2. Choose the current unknown or pressure: timing, method, cost, trust, interpretation or survival, rather than obscuring basic orientation.
3. Alter options or their consequences; then allow reaction, local resolution or a changed kind of pressure to create modulation.
4. Adjust scene duration, summary, paragraph breaks and chapter endings separately. A slow passage may be tense; a fast passage may be light.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Annotate one chapter twice: first for desired/feared outcomes and changing options, then for expansion/compression. Compare the two tracks before revising.

## Applicability and exceptions

The cited model acknowledges debates about uncertainty and individual responses; do not turn it into a numerical suspense score. Constant danger, forced misunderstandings and cliffhangers at every break can undermine the intended experience. Calm, humor and secure relationships can remain valuable.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- What does the reader anticipate, on what evidence, and why care?
- Does increased pressure change the problem rather than merely restate danger?
- Is the pacing choice helping the intended tension or giving needed space to respond?

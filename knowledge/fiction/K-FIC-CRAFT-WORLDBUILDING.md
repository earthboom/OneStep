# Make a world distinctive through connected consequences and lived choices

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

Wrede explicitly frames her question bank as optional and non-exhaustive [S11]; its customs and public-life sections connect setting to everyday and institutional conditions [S12, S13]. Sanderson presents personal heuristics about understandable problem-solving magic, limitations and developing existing elements [S14], while his first-law essay discusses room for unexplained wonder [S15]. These support questions, not mandatory hard-magic design.

### Applied procedure

1. Choose the premise that makes life or perception different here; identify who benefits and who faces a constraint.
2. Trace consequences through one everyday practice, one institution and one contested choice. Ask how people adapt rather than merely naming organizations.
3. For a relevant power, establish capability, access, limit and cost; distinguish what the author knows from what characters and readers have encountered.
4. Reveal setting through necessary action, objects, conflicts or explanation. Keep mysteries whose unknownness serves the intended atmosphere.
5. Check cultural variation within groups and verify real-world historical or scientific claims separately. Stop researching unused details when the current narrative decision is supported.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Pick one invented rule and map its consequences in a household, an organization and a difficult choice. Mark which details must appear now and which remain author-side notes.

## Applicability and exceptions

An encyclopedia of names is not itself evidence of appeal. Realist settings also have institutions and selective disclosure. Do not reproduce historical stereotypes or treat an old fantasy question's example as a verified fact about a real culture. Opaque magic can be intentional; the reader contract determines its role in resolution.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- What choice becomes possible or difficult because of this setting?
- Do everyday practices and institutions respond to the premise consistently?
- Is the needed resolution prepared without explaining away all wonder?

## Evidence

- [S11] [Patricia C. Wrede, Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions, introduction](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-%20questions/) — Author-hosted question bank; opening applicability statements; copyright notice 1996, not a verified original web-publication date.
- [S12] [Patricia C. Wrede, Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions: Peoples and Customs](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/) — Author-hosted questions; Customs, Eating, Greeting/Meeting, Language, Ethics/Values; questions are not anthropological findings.
- [S13] [Patricia C. Wrede, Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions: Commerce, Trade, and Public Life](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/) — Author-hosted questions; trade, industry, transport, communication and medicine; not historical fact-checking.
- [S14] [Brandon Sanderson, What Are Sanderson's Laws Of Magic?](https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/what-are-sandersons-laws-of-magic/) — Author-hosted summary of three personal heuristics; explicitly presented as guidelines, not universal laws.
- [S15] [Brandon Sanderson, Sanderson's First Law](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-first-law) — Author essay, February 2007 second-draft note; Soft Magic, Hard Magic and Middle Ground; scoped to conflict resolution.

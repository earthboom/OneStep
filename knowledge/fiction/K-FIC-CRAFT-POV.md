# Control viewpoint through information access and situated interpretation

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

Niederhoff defines focalization in terms of selecting or restricting narrative information and discusses why knowing no more than a character is not identical to presenting that character's lived perception [S16]. His conceptual distinctions are useful analytical tools; the taxonomy is not a ban on shifts or mixed modes.

### Applied procedure

1. Name the narrator and the local focal person or declared omniscient scope.
2. For consequential claims, distinguish observation, memory, report, inference and privileged narration.
3. Check when each relevant fact becomes available to a character and when it is disclosed to readers.
4. Shape detail and interpretation through the chosen consciousness or distance; signal mode shifts according to the work's established convention.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Mark the information source for five consequential statements in an existing scene. Revise a leak into supported inference, attributed report or a deliberate narratorial shift.

## Applicability and exceptions

First person does not guarantee honesty and third person does not guarantee omniscience. Intentional unreliability, free indirect style and declared omniscience require their own reader cues. Do not misclassify every unexplained fact as a continuity error before checking the narration contract.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- Who can know this here, and by which route?
- Is a character's interpretation being asserted as world fact?
- Can readers recognize the intended scope or shift without access to author notes?

# Turn character knowledge into distinctive desire and consequential action

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

The Open University course uses character sketches across bodily, psychological, interpersonal and historical dimensions, and asks writers to portray the person rather than stop at notes [S04]. This supports using biography as material to test, not filling a dossier as an end in itself.

### Applied procedure

1. Select an immediate want and a value or attachment the person may refuse to sacrifice.
2. Choose the obstacle and their current options, skills and mistaken assumptions; establish motives from the story rather than diagnostic labels.
3. Give a contradiction a situational reason: the same value can produce generosity in one relation and control in another.
4. When significant priorities compete, test the person in a choice with a meaningful cost. In a quiet or exploratory scene, a smaller response, observation or omission can show the same disposition. Track change, resistance or reaffirmed commitment through the cues the scene provides.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Write two brief decisions by the same character under different relationships; annotate the shared motive and the different behavior. Keep only profile details that help explain or productively complicate the scenes.

### Compare responses and trace what the reader sees

Hold the situation or goal constant when comparing two characters. Give each a plausible way to proceed, then ask what each protects and what each risks losing. Compare one character across two relationships or kinds of pressure when the apparent contradiction matters. Record the cue where the reader encounters it. A later exception can revise the earlier impression if the reader can see what has changed, at the point the work intends them to know it.

For example, a storekeeper signs a reprimand in front of their superior. In private, they slide the torn ledger back to the apprentice and ask them to repair it. Signing makes shared responsibility visible. Handing back the ledger suggests that the apprentice still has a task to do. The sequence could express care or ordinary supervisory duty. If care must be clear, compare how the same person handles a comparable mistake by a different apprentice, and give the reader a concrete detail that supports the intended distinction.

To draft a subtle or quiet passage, choose what the viewpoint character attends to, or what the character seeks from a listener. When writing Korean speech, use [the Korean voice checks](K-FIC-KO-VOICE.md) for address, endings, and the relationship or change that motivates them. For a draft comparison, ask someone who has only read the relevant passages what mattered to the character, which words or actions support it, and what else the scene could mean. Use the answer to locate an unclear cue or a supported alternative reading.

## Applicability and exceptions

Interest is not the same as kindness or a universal competence formula. A static arc, constrained person or opaque motive may be intentional. Trauma, quirks and contradictory traits alone do not demonstrate depth or cause a specific reader reaction.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- Can this choice be understood from what this person wants and knows now?
- Would another character with a different history plausibly act or speak the same way here?
- Is the intended attraction or discomfort supported by the actual scene rather than a profile adjective?

### Supplementary evidence checked on 2026-11-26

- [Mischel and Shoda, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.246), opening sections and model: personality theory describes patterns in how behavior varies with the person's reading of a situation. Comparing a fictional character's responses is an editorial application.
- [Rapp, Gerrig and Prentice, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmla.2000.2789), experiments and general discussion, and [Rapp and Kendeou, 2007](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03192934.pdf), experiment 2: readers of short English narratives used relevant trait cues and could update earlier inferences when given an explanation. The studies address comprehension and expectations. The particular writing procedure above has yet to be tested with manuscript readers.
- [Jannidis, Character](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/41.html), Characterization section, and [Lee Sang-il, 2021](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002804218), abstract: direct and inferential characterization are available, and a Korean literature education proposal selects and contextualizes decisive cues using *Simcheongjeon* as an example. The proposed review questions are an adaptation.
- [Sanderson, 2025 lecture 6](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/customizing-your-character-brandon-sandersons-writing-lecture-6-2025), official notes on motivation, personality, and values, and [Saunders, Consistency of Tone](https://georgesaunders.substack.com/p/consistency-of-tone), same-room example: practitioner demonstrations of different approaches to the same goal and different narration of the same place. [Kim Jung-hyuk's interview](https://ch.yes24.com/article/details/35120) discusses a Korean novelist's dialogue revisions, rhythm, and how a speaker's hesitation to assert certainty can show in sentence endings.
- [Le Guin, Steering the Craft](https://lithub.com/a-writing-lesson-from-ursula-k-leguin/), published chapter 1 excerpt, uses rhythm to embody action or emotion. [Yoon and Han, 2020](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002633566), abstract, analyzes addressee speech levels in *Mujeong* by hierarchy, intimacy, and formality. It supplies questions for checking shifts in address, with the usage findings scoped to that historical novel.

# Distinguish rewarding stylistic effort from obstructive prose

## Guidance

P09 argues for foregrounding, affect and personal interpretation beyond propositional comprehension; it reports earlier empirical associations rather than an independent replication. P16 concerns situation tracking, not a prescription for vivid writing. Treat the following as editorial choices.

Identify the narrator's baseline: diction, perceived detail, emotional explanation, sentence movement and dialogue/action balance. Ask what a conspicuous metaphor, repetition, omission or pause contributes here. Preserve it if it makes an experience perceptible or changes interpretation in a way this work needs. If it only repeats an already clear handoff or explanation, compare a leaner alternative.

Read the paragraph with the preceding and following exchanges. Trace referents, actions, information access and the placement of emphasis, then read aloud when sound matters. Do not replace a character-specific line with a portable maxim merely because it sounds polished. A strong detail can serve atmosphere or thought without advancing plot.

### Korean-language comparison

For Korean-specific evidence, use the [expression guide](K-FIC-KO-EXPRESSION-GUIDE.md) and retrieve the relevant topic rather than generalizing English sentence habits. They are illustrations; their effect was not measured. Word affect norms, phonological associations and grammatical stance do not replace a contextual reading.

## Applicability and exceptions

Fast reading is not automatically good; prolonged attention is not automatically confusion. Conversely, difficulty is not proof of depth. Distinguish a productive question about meaning from inability to tell who acts or speaks. No universal sentence length, metaphor quota or ban on repeated words follows from the research. Korean prosody and genre-specific voice require actual Korean prose checks.

## Checks

- What does the reader gain for the attention this sentence asks?
- Does the unusual phrase fit this narrator or speaker?
- Is an explanation repeating a cue the action already supplies?
- If compressed, are a needed transition and the intended pause still legible?
- Is the revision preferable in context, rather than only shorter?

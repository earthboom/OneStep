# Diagnose engagement as several reader experiences

## Guidance

P03 develops a multidimensional self-report approach for textual narratives. P02 studies transportation and persuasion. Use their distinction as a diagnostic lens, not a manuscript score.

For an observed weak point, name the experience at issue: following the scene, maintaining attention, imagining its situation, caring about an outcome, or enjoying its presentation. Ask what the passage gives the reader to attend to and why it matters now. A vivid setting can coexist with weak concern for the characters; easy comprehension can coexist with indifference.

Choose the smallest intervention matched to the diagnosis. Clarify a missing referent for confusion; make a consequential choice legible for weak emotional stakes; remove redundant explanation for a stalled exchange. These are candidate edits, not effects guaranteed by the papers. Keep the draft's desired distance: observational narration need not place readers inside every character.

## Applicability and exceptions

Absorption is not the same as truth, ethical approval, meaning or commercial retention. Critical distance, irony and deliberate reflection may interrupt smooth immersion productively. A reader with little visual imagery may still respond intensely. Do not require vivid internal pictures or continuous arousal in every scene.

An informal Korean questionnaire inspired by these dimensions is a custom feedback instrument, not a validated translation of SWAS. Do not reproduce a scale, invent a score or borrow its validation without checking the original measure and usage conditions.

## Checks

- Is the finding about comprehension, attention, emotion, imagery or an outcome such as enjoyment?
- Is there a cited passage or an actual reader report, rather than an assumed response?
- Does the proposed edit serve the work's intended closeness or distance?
- Does clearer exposition cost mystery, rhythm or interpretive room?
- Compare versions on the selected dimension and preserve successful passages.

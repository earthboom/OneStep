# Shape Korean character voice through knowledge, endings and relational stance

## Guidance

K07 analyzes sources of knowledge, K08 reports corpus functions of endings, and K09-K10 concern literary dialogue and translation. Use their questions to inspect utterances in a relationship, not to replace the character's established voice with a grammar demonstration.

Record who speaks to whom, what is newly perceived or inferred, what response is sought and what social or emotional distance is being maintained. Compare the same proposition with another ending, then read the response. Recognition, assertion, invitation, guarded acceptance or sarcasm require context; they cannot be assigned from the suffix alone.

Keep subject honorification distinct from the level used toward an addressee. A formal ending can be routine, protective, respectful or hostile in different exchanges. A shift toward informality can express intimacy or aggression, depending on the relationship and trigger. Preserve meaningful inconsistency, such as a person holding a professional register under pressure, when supported by the text.

A one-word change may alter stance without changing propositional content; other changes may alter the social relation itself. Mark which is being compared.

## Applicability and exceptions

K07's analysis and K08's corpus discussion operate at different explanatory levels; do not infer that -네 always requires direct witnessing or -구나 always denotes a specific emotion. Corpus prevalence does not prove a historical voice is inappropriate, or that a particular ending is touching.

K09 analyzes selected translated fiction, not all everyday conversation or original Korean novels. Speech-level stereotypes based only on age, gender or genre are insufficient. Period dialogue, dialect and a real person's voice need appropriate sources.

## Checks

- Does the line match the speaker's available knowledge and intended action?
- Are honorification of the subject and address to the listener distinguished?
- Does an ending change have a relationship or situation trigger?
- Are different characters' registers distinguishable without artificial catchphrases?
- Did a rewrite remove speaker-identifying cues and make the exchange ambiguous?

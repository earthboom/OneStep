# Evaluate Korean emotional wording through sense, context and reader appraisal

## Guidance

K04 develops word-level norms, K03 analyzes bodily idioms, K14 theorizes reader emotion in poetry, and K16 distinguishes dictionary senses. These are different kinds of evidence. None assigns an inevitable reader response to a sentence.

Identify the word's local sense, whose emotion is represented and what the event means to that person. Valence, intensity and a word's concreteness are separate questions. A remembered reassuring phrase can become distressing after a changed relationship; the same expression can accompany anger, relief or grief. Do not infer a paragraph's emotional effect by adding positive and negative words.

Use bodily sensations when this viewpoint notices them and they serve the event. A throat, hand or breath cue can be literal, idiomatic or figurative; avoid diagnosing a character or a reader from it. Sometimes the exact emotion word is clearer than another generic bodily reaction.

For uncertainty, ask what a reader understands or imagines and which passage prompted it, before asking for an emotion label. Preserve plausible different responses.

## Applicability and exceptions

K04's raw 450-word norms were not downloaded here. Do not fabricate ratings, sample totals or a sentiment classifier. Its Korean and English participant descriptions require reconciliation before detailed reuse. K14 is theoretical and educational, not a controlled test of reader tears or empathy.

Avoid treating one vocabulary set as the authentic Korean heart, or equating Han, Jeong, native words or Sino-Korean words with a fixed aesthetic superiority. Words and cultural associations belong to speakers, histories and contexts. A dictionary definition supports sense selection, not literary approval.

## Checks

- Is a lexical emotion being confused with the reader's emotion?
- Does context establish why this cue matters?
- Are bodily descriptions repetitive or doing specific work?
- Could the word have another relevant sense here?
- Which claim is text-supported and which requires actual reader evidence?

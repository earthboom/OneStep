# Build Korean figurative expression from a scene-specific relation

## Guidance

K01 concerns rhetorical classifications, K02 metaphor processing, K03 bodily idioms, and K12 metaphor extension in poetry reading. Their outcomes are not measures of a novel's movingness. Use them to frame the following editorial choices.

Start with what the person experiences: a delayed apology, an unwelcome realization, a familiar absence, a brief joy. Choose a concrete relation that the scene and viewpoint can sustain. A simile makes comparison overt; metaphor can be predicative or verbal rather than only the school shorthand A is B. Metonymy can direct attention through a meaningful adjacency, such as a person's habitual object or bodily action; it need not assert a resemblance.

For cross-sensory language, identify which quality passes between senses and why this perceiver notices it now. Literary synaesthesia does not establish a neurological condition. A conventional expression can be apt; making every phrase unprecedented can obscure the situation. Trace an extended image through consequences and changes rather than merely accumulating matching nouns.

Separate the mechanism from those sample sentences. Their words are not defaults for a new character.

## Applicability and exceptions

Rhetorical taxonomies differ; do not make a label the main purpose of revision. Metonymy, symbol and metaphor can overlap in a literary reading. A prop's presence alone does not make it a symbol. Nor is a mixed metaphor automatically an error: test whether the actual combination remains interpretable and purposeful.

K02 compares linguistic processing, not beautiful versus ugly fiction. K03 analyzes selected emotional idioms for language teaching; bodily expressions are neither diagnostic signs nor a uniform Korean psychology. K12 concerns a specific poem and expert response texts; transferring its questions to prose is an editorial choice.

## Checks

- What shared feature or adjacency links the concrete expression and experience?
- Is the source material accessible to this character, moment and genre?
- Does the image retain a legible literal scene around it?
- Does its continuation change interpretation or only repeat decoration?
- Would plain statement convey the needed feeling more precisely here?

# Separate suspense, curiosity, surprise and disclosure decisions

## Guidance

P04 proposes a model of consequential anticipation. P05 examines suspense across outcome knowledge and rereading. P15 examines enjoyment under spoiler conditions. Together they caution against treating ignorance, suspense and pleasure as one variable.

As an editorial distinction, ask three separate questions: what future outcome is hoped for or feared (suspense); what missing cause or explanation is sought (curiosity); what event revises an expectation (surprise)? They can coexist. For each important beat, record the reader's live question, known possibilities, personal stakes and what new information changes. Do not impose a fixed numerical curve.

Test disclosure by alternatives: conceal an answer while keeping the immediate action intelligible; disclose a danger that the character does not know; or let the reader know the outcome while uncertain about the route or cost. Choose from the current genre and scene purpose. Preserve earned setup and payoff; an arbitrary withheld fact is not automatically a useful mystery.

## Applicability and exceptions

P05 is a small, narrow experiment; P15 measures enjoyment and distinguishes external spoilers from editing story openings. These are not instructions to spoil every mystery or guarantees that rereading increases tension. A first-read revelation still matters when the work promises it. Quiet scenes can sustain interest through intimacy, observation, humor or thought rather than danger.

## Checks

- What does the reader anticipate, and why should the alternatives matter?
- Is confusion caused by a missing action link being mistaken for suspense?
- Does a reveal change choices, costs or interpretation?
- Does the new beat earn its payoff without breaking a rule?
- Did speeding up sentences remove preparation or a useful pause?

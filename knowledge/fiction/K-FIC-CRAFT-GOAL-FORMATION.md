# Trace how a character acquires a long-term goal

## Guidance

For scenes on either side of a new goal, identify four things separately: the intended direction of the arc, what the character wants now, the goal or plan the character has formed, and what the reader can infer. Before the new commitment, ask which experiences change what the character knows, wants or believes is possible. Locate the first available choice that puts that change into action.

Recheck an early scene with the eventual objective covered up. If the action still makes sense from the character's immediate concerns and relationships, preserve that motive. The outcome can prepare a later decision without making the character deliberately pursue that decision in the earlier scene. Recheck scene notes, summaries and references after revising the timeline so an old motive does not survive in another writing input.

## Applicability and exceptions

Use when a goal forms during the story or when outline intent risks leaking into a character's motives. A story may open with a formed goal, or let the reader recognize a direction before the character does. Establish each at the level the scene actually supports. This check does not prescribe a delay or require a declaration in dialogue.

## Checks

- Identify an early action with an intelligible current motive and a later decision with an observable intervening cause.
- Identify what the character can know or intend at each point and what the reader has evidence to infer.
- Reopen linked outlines and notes before drafting to confirm that they agree on the goal's earliest supported stage.

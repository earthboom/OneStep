# Evaluate meaning through choices, costs and interpretive aftereffects

## Guidance

P10 distinguishes appreciation from other entertainment responses in film research. P11 offers a theoretical account of negative emotions in art. These support asking different questions about pleasure and reflection; they do not validate a particular theme or mandate suffering.

As an editorial procedure, state a live value tension in the work: what two worthwhile commitments, desires or interpretations cannot be reconciled cheaply? Locate choices and costs that give each side substance. Track how repeated actions, images or relationship turns acquire a changed meaning by the ending. Consider an alternative reading and the passages supporting it.

Check whether an explicit thematic line adds something the actions have not already earned. A plain statement can be appropriate to a speaker and scene; subtext is not universally superior. Preserve unresolved questions when the work supports them. Meaning can arise through humor, care, ordinary life, wonder or formal experience as well as catastrophe.

## Applicability and exceptions

Appreciation is a reader response, not a certificate of profundity. P10's medium differs from a novel; P11 is a model, not a dose-response experiment on cruelty. Neither justifies equating pain, moral ambiguity, complexity or pessimism with quality. A satisfying resolution need not settle a philosophical dispute. A character's claim remains that character's claim unless the work establishes more.

## Checks

- Which actual choice and consequence embody the proposed theme?
- Does the opposing value have substance, or is it made conveniently foolish?
- Does a repeated image change meaning, or merely repeat?
- Is an ending's explanation closing interpretive room the work needs?
- What is supported by textual analysis, and what aftereffect requires reader evidence?

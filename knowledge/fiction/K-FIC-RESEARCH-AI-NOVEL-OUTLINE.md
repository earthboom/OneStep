# Design a whole novel scenario with AI and review the outline before drafting

## Guidance

### Choose the method by the design problem

The international evidence below covers several separable design problems. An overall outline provides direction, causal links explain why the next event can happen, and a state record shows what has changed by a specific chapter. Pair these when they answer different questions about the same story.

| If the main difficulty is… | Try this in OneStep | Evidence and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A weak climax or too few alternatives | Start with two or three materially different climaxes. Work backward to required information, motive and resources, then forward through costs, consequences and the ending. For a discovery-led story, alternate open exploration with review of current candidates. | IPOCL and Haslum support explicit action prerequisites and intentions. BiT-MCTS explores around a theme-defining climax. MEXICA alternates exploration and reflection on short story frameworks [N01, G01, G09, G14]. |
| Parallel arcs that feel disconnected | Give each major thread its own unresolved question and progression. Try alternation, a shared person or consequence, or a story nested inside another. Mark the exact event where one arc changes another's available choices. Check actual occurrence order separately from the order the reader encounters it. | Plot combination tests and Japanese structural prototypes offer concrete composition patterns [N06, G05, G07, G08]. |
| A long series that forgets promises or knowledge | Retain the overall direction and plan the active arc in detail. Keep established facts, local state changes and open obligations with chapter or passage links. Before a later scene, retrieve only what that scene needs. Confirm each new fact against the revised manuscript before using it downstream. If a conflict is intended, record the reason. | DOME, TRACE and NstAgent motivate different memory/revision designs. TRACE gives the author control of audits, and NstAgent documents a case where an incorrect extraction became entrenched [N05, G03, G04]. |
| A coherent but predictable or culturally thin plot | Compare variants by character values, the resulting sacrifices, and how relationships shape what can be said or done. Ask someone familiar with the target language and social context to read the pivotal scenes. Use the story's own genre, cultural cues and emotional goals as criteria. | Brazilian telenovela comparison, Arabic and African-language story experiments, and African community evaluation probe different parts of this issue [G10–G13]. Their formats and evaluators differ from Korean novel readers. |
| Unclear whether the design improved the work | Review outline causality and disclosure on their own, test pivotal scenes in prose, and record reader responses when available. Use fast summaries to locate problems and original passages to resolve specific findings. Compare alternatives under the same premise, length and constraints. | Outline evaluation, WebNovelBench, LongStoryEval and the Melbourne study measure different outcomes under different conditions [N11, G02, G15, G16]. |

For a practical layout, choose a chapter outline with editable scene summaries and a small set of attached reference records. The product and project documents at the end provide interface ideas for this layout. Their feature descriptions can help reproduce a workflow, and research results remain attached to their own methods and conditions.

Choose the amount of planning by the rework a decision could cause. A mystery with several dependent reveals benefits from an early causal chain and a disclosure table. A discovery-led novel can keep several endings open and plan the next arc in detail. For either mode, preserve the writer's decisions and use the AI to propose alternatives, expose dependencies and revise the affected area. Re3, DOC and DOME illustrate different ways of combining a global plan with local context. Dramatron and Fabula illustrate ways writers can retain access to and edit the intermediate artifacts [N02–N05, N10].

| Decision | What to ask the AI to produce | What to inspect before expanding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set a direction | A premise with the reader promise, genre and tone, whose goal drives events, what pressures it, and the consequences of distinct ending options. Mark author decisions, tentative proposals and open questions. | Do the options change a choice, cost or implication? Which choices can remain open? Compare a few short options before committing to detailed versions. |
| Build the whole plot | A synopsis from opening to ending plus the causal spine of consequential events. For each turning point, give the trigger, character goal, available information, choice, cost, changed state and dependent event. Carry the main plot, significant relationship arcs, and subplots and their intersections. | Can the protagonist and opposing characters make these choices with their own motives and means? Trace the ending backward for missing prerequisites, then trace the opening forward for unearned pivots [N01, N06]. |
| Arrange the telling | Assign event IDs to chapters and specify chronology, chapter/scene placement, viewpoint and information released to each character and to the reader. Record the initial question, its intermediate answers and the setup/payoff links. | For a flashback or reveal, check both what actually happened and why the reader learns it here. For a serial, check what changes in each installment and which earlier promise it advances [N06]. |
| Detail the active arc | Expand chapters into scene cards using the current outline, confirmed world rules, relevant relationships and state before the scene. Record the viewpoint character's immediate goal, obstacle, decision or discovery, and what changes by the end. | Can the scenes support the intended atmosphere and conflict? A quiet chapter can change understanding, closeness or expectation. Keep voice and prose checks for the manuscript [N03, N09]. |
| Reconcile changes | After a proposed change, list affected events, later scenes, character knowledge, relationships and setup/payoff pairs with their IDs and the reason each depends on it. Record the new outline version and unresolved alternatives. | Check both prerequisites and downstream consequences. Compare revised and previous versions. Scope new requests to the relevant plan, facts and source passages [N02, N05, N08]. |

### Keep the shared state small

Start with two files or sections: a versioned whole-work outline with a chapter table, and a continuity table. A row in the continuity table can record an event ID, story time and chapter of disclosure, participant IDs, the before/after change, what each affected character and the reader can know, and links to dependent events, clues or payoffs. Mark whether a row is an author decision, draft proposal, in-world claim or manuscript-supported fact, and attach the source when available. If a passage confirms only a belief, keep it attributed. Use the [relationship-map method](K-FIC-CRAFT-RELATION-MAP.md) for stable IDs and directional evidence. Markdown and tables are sufficient for the initial trial. Add JSON or retrieval tooling when repeated manual lookup creates friction.

### Review design and prose at their own scale

For a design decision, request a finding with event/scene IDs, conflicting or missing evidence, likely consequence and smallest repair. If an apparent gap may be an intentional secret or ambiguity, state what a reader can infer by the specified chapter and what evidence would decide the issue. Inspect the synopsis by reading through the whole cause-and-effect chain in order, and inspect chapter/scene transitions separately. The existing [plausibility method](K-FIC-PLAUSIBILITY-REVIEW.md) gives evidence standards for character choice and information access.

When drafting begins, test a scene that carries a costly choice or a difficult reveal. Compare the prose to the scene goal, viewpoint, established state and intended tone. A trial of this kind can help choose how tightly to specify later scenes. If comparing AI approaches in one project, hold premise, constraints and target scope constant. Compare a direct outline with a staged outline and keep separate results for design defects, editing effort and readers' reactions to the prose. Record scope, model and human review conditions when running a trial. Studies that rate expanded stories or extract outlines from finished books answer different questions [N03, N11, N12].

## Applicability and exceptions

This procedure is a project adaptation. The source studies range from formal narrative plans and screenplay collaboration through several-thousand-word stories, plot outlines and book-scale training. Their lengths, genres, inputs, judges and tasks differ. Transfer to a full Korean novel, including reader engagement, requires project-specific review. A staged outline can impose a formula when the writer is still discovering the work. Keep turning points provisional where appropriate, distinguish a deliberate open ending from an accidental dropped thread, and use genre templates when they fit the project.

Formal planning helps specify prerequisites and consequences. Human editorial judgment is still required for the emotional credibility of a choice, the force of a theme or the distinctiveness of an ending. Model critique can locate places to inspect. Carry source evidence and uncertainty into any correction, especially when one model both generates and judges the work.

## Checks

- Are the ending or deliberately open question and the opening promise visible in the synopsis?
- Does each decisive choice follow from an actor's goal, information, means and pressure? If the choice changes, what later events would change?
- Are world chronology and reveal order separate where they differ? What can the reader and the viewpoint character each know by this chapter?
- Does each major setup have an intended payoff, deliberate deferral or decision to remove it? Do subplots affect the main plot, characters or theme in a traceable way?
- Do active chapters and scenes have identifiable consequences? Do planned and manuscript-confirmed states remain distinguishable after changes?
- Are design findings, prose findings and actual reader feedback recorded with their own evidence?

## Evidence

Primary sources checked on 2026-11-19. The procedure above adapts their methods and limitations. Counts and outcomes refer to the cited studies' tasks.

The initial source set is N01–N12. The international expansion used English, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Arabic and Hindi search terms, with additional searches on African-language storytelling and Australian research. G01–G16 are selected original studies with relevant methods or transfer boundaries. This is a targeted international review. The search does not estimate the number of eligible papers or guarantee country-by-country coverage. Author affiliation, language of the paper, language of the story and evaluation population are treated separately.

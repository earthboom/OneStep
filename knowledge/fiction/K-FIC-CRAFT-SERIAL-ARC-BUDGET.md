# Budget a serial arc by consequential turns

## Guidance

Record the author's target range or minimum for installments, the unit of length, any allowed variation, and the work the arc must accomplish. Reserve space for the independent cast threads, their smaller completed events, accumulating clues, crisis escalation, response and aftermath. Give each major block a before-and-after change and carry the outcome into the next block or another thread.

For a long pursuit, journey or siege, keep elapsed story time separate from reading space. Check what changes available information, resources, relationships, options or consequences in each block. A changed location or another confrontation can matter if it changes a later choice. Merge or summarize repeated movement and repeated briefings when the underlying choices and stakes remain the same. A quiet recovery can earn space through a new cost, decision or relationship change.

## Applicability and exceptions

Apply when the author has a concrete long-serial scale and several threads to develop. Chapter allocations are candidates until the material has been drafted. The source case supplies a way to audit allocation and repetition; its chapter counts and ratios are specific to that work.

## Checks

- Compare the estimate with the requested installment scale and distinguish a per-installment minimum from a fixed length.
- Identify an early local result and a later use for each major supporting thread; preserve breathing room for aftermath.
- Track chronology separately. Compare consecutive blocks with the same task to see whether their information, options, costs or outcomes change.
- Recheck the actual chapters for pacing once prose exists.

# Choose Korean ideophones by lexical sense, perception and scene register

## Guidance

K15 explains sound-symbolic patterns; K05 and K06 study different perceptual or processing tasks. Their results caution against treating bright/dark-vowel terminology as a fixed positive/negative emotion code.

First choose the event: sound, motion, texture, bodily sensation or manner. Check a candidate's lexical senses, what it conventionally denotes and which intensity or scale fits. Compare words in the actual sentence, including consonants, vowel pattern, repetition and suffixes; do not mechanically replace vowels to invent an attested word. A familiar pair such as 반짝반짝/번쩍번쩍 need not differ only in an abstract emotional sign.

Ask whether the perceiver would notice that sound and whether its rhythm suits the scene. One ideophone may make an action concrete; removing it may better preserve distance or restraint. Read the surrounding prose, not an isolated list of attractive words.

## Applicability and exceptions

K05 reports favorable judgments of certain sound classes in its task, while K06 distinguishes explicit associations from mixed online processing results. These are different measures and stimuli, not a simple contradiction or a universal rule. Neither establishes that a specific sound moves novel readers. Native and non-native judgments can rely on different information.

K16's dictionary discussion helps distinguish senses of 울컥 and related classifications; it does not settle all ideophones' grammatical behavior. Do not make every action noisy, childlike or comic, or assume ideophones are intrinsically unsuitable for serious prose. Determine register from the work.

## Checks

- Does the word denote the intended event, sense and scale?
- Is an alleged word a genuine usage or only an invented vowel substitution?
- Does sound repetition change duration or texture the scene needs?
- Are several neighboring sound effects competing for attention?
- Did the edit improve the passage's perception or merely decorate it?

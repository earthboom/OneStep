# Check causal and situational continuity without flattening narrative form

## Guidance

P01 models event connections along five dimensions; P16 concerns neural tracking of changing story situations. Neither establishes a formula for reader enjoyment. The following is an editorial application.

For a confusing event or transition, record: reader-visible state before -> actor's available information and aim -> action -> changed state -> next dependency. Track time, place, person, causal connection and intention only where consequential. In a speculative world, distinguish an unfamiliar rule from a contradiction of an established rule. Supply a cue at the first action that needs it, rather than front-loading the entire world's history.

Use a small event map for a disputed passage: which earlier condition makes this action possible, which choice changes it, and what becomes possible or costly afterward? Separate temporal succession from a supported causal claim. Relationships can supply causes through trust, duty, deception or incompatible aims; do not reduce all choices to efficient problem-solving.

## Applicability and exceptions

A fragmented, unreliable, dreamlike or quiet narrative can deliberately leave links unsettled. Check its intended form and available information before diagnosing a gap. Not every detail needs a plot consequence: atmosphere, rhythm and ordinary life have functions. These papers do not demonstrate that complete explanation, maximum causal density or more sensory detail makes a novel better.

## Checks

- Can the reader locate the consequential action with what has actually been disclosed?
- Does a resolution use a rule, possession or piece of knowledge not yet established?
- Can an apparent contradiction be explained by viewpoint or intentional uncertainty supported in the text?
- Would the smallest movement or information cue repair the gap without spoiling a reveal?
- After repair, follow affected props, knowledge and rules to their next use.

# Compose sentences by meaning, rhythm and the work's voice

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

Le Guin's publisher-supplied chapter excerpt emphasizes hearing prose and attending to its sound and movement [S10]. The Open University editing lesson connects sentence-level clarity with purpose, voice and larger revision decisions [S18]. English-language examples supply craft questions, not Korean word-order or punctuation rules.

### Applied procedure

1. Establish the intended meaning and who perceives or speaks. Check referents and the order of perception, thought and action.
2. Choose where to expand, delay, repeat or stop according to the scene's attention and emotional movement.
3. Read the paragraph aloud or listen internally; examine monotonous endings, unintended ambiguity and a voice that changes without reason.
4. Revise only what impairs the intended meaning or effect. Reread the surrounding paragraph and preserve deliberate repetition, ambiguity or long syntax.

### Korean-specific reference route

Added 2026-11-17: consult the [Korean expression guide](K-FIC-KO-EXPRESSION-GUIDE.md) for figuration, sound symbolism, endings, recoverable omission, repetition and emotional word senses. It supplies Korean-focused evidence alongside the English craft teaching, with source types and limits attached. Compare choices against this speaker and scene, not a list of inherently moving words. The original S10/S18 source-check dates remain unchanged.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Take one paragraph and write versions that emphasize immediacy and deliberation. Annotate the clause order and pauses that change; choose by the current work's effect, not by which is shortest.

## Applicability and exceptions

No fixed sentence-length distribution, adverb prohibition or mandatory showing rule establishes good prose. Korean register, omitted subjects, particles and endings require Korean contextual judgment. Loud reading is a diagnostic, not an objective beauty test.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- Can the intended reader follow who does or perceives what?
- Does sound and clause movement fit this voice and moment?
- Did the edit preserve meaning, character and useful rhythm rather than only reduce words?

# Build anticipation through changing information, costs and earned payoff

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

Sanderson's official plot notes connect narrative expectations to development and payoff [S02]. Writing Excuses distinguishes elapsed story time from perceived progress toward what readers are following [S06]. This gives a basis for diagnosing a busy middle that changes little, without requiring a surprise ending.

### Applied procedure

1. Name the open question, emotional expectation or capability on which a later moment depends.
2. List prerequisite facts, skills, relationships and costs; record when viewpoint character and reader can encounter each.
3. Make successive beats change a hypothesis, option, cost, relationship or feasible outcome. Repetition should add pressure, interpretation or consequence.
4. At payoff, connect the decisive choice to what was available earlier. Resolve local promises and deliberately manage what remains open across chapters.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Choose a future reversal and trace it backward to three prerequisites, then trace the actual reader-facing cues forward. This is an authored exercise; three is a small review sample, not a foreshadowing quota.

## Applicability and exceptions

Preparation is not explaining the answer early, and withholding is not hiding information a chosen narrator ought to disclose without reason. Known outcomes can still carry tension. For deduction-based mysteries also consult the existing Knox reference with its exceptions.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- What has actually changed between successive preparation beats?
- Could the payoff be understood using information available before it?
- Which questions are answered now and which have an intentional later horizon?

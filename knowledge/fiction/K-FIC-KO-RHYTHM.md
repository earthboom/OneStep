# Control Korean omission and repetition without losing the reader

## Guidance

K10-K11 examine literary translation and K13 proposes a poetic-rhythm pedagogy. They supply questions about what remains recoverable and what repeated structure does. Their transfer to original prose is editorial, not an established universal recipe.

Before omitting a subject, object or attribution, identify its recoverable referent and the next possible competitor. A repeated subject may be unnecessary or may emphasize agency, distance or insistence. Do not remove it by rule. Distinguish a grammatically recoverable element from a whole action or emotional turn the reader has not been given.

Compare clause and paragraph placement around perception, thought, action and response. Keep a pause if it changes anticipation or leaves interpretive room; remove a redundant transfer explanation when adjacent actions already supply it. Repetition can create accumulation or a changed final element, but repeated syntax without a function can flatten a passage.

Compare versions with their surroundings, not by character count alone. For deliberate lyrical prose, read aloud while checking the underlying action remains clear.

## Applicability and exceptions

A poem's line break is not equivalent to every prose paragraph break. K13's argument does not establish mandatory syllable counts. K11's reported translation proportions are observations, not target omission rates. The current speaker, viewpoint and genre determine what to retain.

Restraint is not a requirement to hide every feeling. Direct emotional statement, explicit attribution and repeated naming can be precise choices. A missing causal link is not automatically a productive silence.

## Checks

- Can the reader recover the omitted referent from available context?
- Has another actor or possible antecedent intervened?
- Does repetition accumulate, contrast or shift emphasis?
- Does the final clause land on the intended perception or consequence?
- After compression, are action, speaker and the useful pause preserved?

# Revise from intended experience to structural and sentence decisions

## Guidance

### Source-supported basis

The Open University treats editing as clarifying intention through choices at sentence and larger scales [S18]. Kim Yeonsu's direct interview acknowledges uncertainty about how an intended world meets readers [S19]. Neither supports treating an author's intention or an AI's agreement as proof of the effect on actual readers.

### Applied procedure

1. State the intended experience and preservation conditions, then read the actual affected draft in full.
2. Repair the earliest supported issue in motivation, causal sequence, information access or emotional preparation before polishing dependent sentences.
3. Separate a reader's reported confusion, boredom or reaction from the specific rewrite they suggest. Decide using the work's aim and evidence.
4. Reread changed passages and their dependencies for voice, clarity and rhythm; collect specific reader responses when an audience-effect claim matters.

### Research-informed evaluation

For a draft-level diagnosis, use [K-FIC-EVALUATION](K-FIC-EVALUATION.md) and the [scholarly research map](K-FIC-RESEARCH-GUIDE.md), added on 2026-11-17. The evaluation profile separates enjoyment, engagement, character, tension, style, world coherence and meaning. Ground each material finding in the inspected draft, consider an alternative reading, name the gain and tradeoff of a small revision, and check preservation afterward. Source evidence and reader response remain separate; no universal quality score or mandatory beta-reader gate is introduced. The original S18/S19 source inspection date remains 2026-11-14.

### Exercise and expected artifact

Choose one reader-facing problem, retain the original, revise the smallest useful span and reread the surrounding exchange. Record what changed and which intended effect remains untested.

## Applicability and exceptions

Revision order is a heuristic; discovery writing may need sentence work to find the story. A polished fragment is not proof that the whole chapter works. No fixed draft count or majority-reader claim follows from these sources. The existing manuscript-review reference remains the workflow authority.

Use the current manuscript, author choices and available context as the authority for application. This entry supplies options for a concrete decision, not requirements to apply every device.

## Checks

- Does the change address an observed passage-level issue or only a taste difference?
- Which later facts, reactions or disclosures did the change invalidate?
- What is verified by rereading, and what still needs a real reader?

# Design learnable and accessible interaction across game genres

## Guidance

Source observations: XAG 103 recommends communicating critical information through another sensory channel [S1]. XAG 107 covers remapping and alternatives to demanding input patterns [S2]. XAG 109 addresses reviewable objectives and instruction beyond static control diagrams [S3]. XAG 108 separates experienced difficulty into player capability and game barriers [S4].

Proposed use: map first launch, settings, initial action, failure, return after a break and late-game demands. For each necessary action, list what must be perceived, understood, remembered and performed. Preserve the intended challenge while identifying avoidable sensory, cognitive or motor barriers. Specify how altered bindings, subtitles, cue channels and assistance affect tutorials and the rest of the interface.

Prefer tasks that reveal whether players recognize the current goal and act independently. For discovery-focused games, offer layered or optional help so access to essential interaction need not disclose the puzzle answer.

## Applicability and exceptions

Apply the relevant guideline sections to the actual mechanic and audience; do not impose one difficulty count or erase intended challenge. Local competitive assistance may require fairness review. A high-contrast screenshot, automated check or disabled sensory channel alone cannot establish accessibility for all players. Source videos and game examples were not played in this task.

## Checks

Proposed checks: can players reach settings without first crossing the barrier? Do prompts follow remapped inputs? Can critical cues be understood without color alone or sound alone? Can returning players recover goals without replaying everything? Test the relevant complete journey and report participant/device/settings scope; keep optional hints distinct from mandatory spoilers.

# Separate build identity, loot evaluation, and upgrade investment

## Guidance

**Source observations.** Blizzard's 2020 design update paired class identity with customization and described the tension between experimenting and committing through respec costs. Its 2024 overhaul simplified dropped affixes, shifted customization into crafting, and reduced drops while adjusting salvage materials. These are different development stages, not one stable specification.

**Proposed reusable method.** Draw the loop as encounter → reward recognition → comparison → equip/salvage → investment → changed combat options. For each transition, identify the player's question, information shown, resource spent, and recovery path. Separate three budgets: time spent evaluating drops, power gained, and cost of changing direction. A reduction in drop count must therefore be checked against crafting supply as well as excitement.

Make a progression sheet for early learning, build formation, and endgame refinement. Assign each tier a new decision or capability; explain numerical increases that introduce no new behavior. Record which power comes from character choices, equipment, and their interaction. For a proposed respec restriction, state the intended commitment and the experiment it might prevent. Schedule an iteration pass before authoring the full item pool, since affix changes affect tooltips, crafting, encounter requirements, and rewards.

## Applicability and exceptions

Use for repeatable combat with build experimentation and loot investment. Narrative RPG progression, party development, MMORPG social economies, and monetization need additional evidence. Permanent character identity can justify costly changes; a short experimental run may favor easy revision. Neither source establishes an ideal respec price, drop rate, or affix count. The 2020 proposal must not be presented as current Diablo IV behavior.

## Checks

- Ask a new tester to explain an upgrade and its combat effect without external guides; record comparison time and misunderstood conditions.
- Simulate several build changes using the actual currency, salvage, and upgrade rules; inspect scarcity and irreversible mistakes.
- Test a weak but appealing build against intended encounters, then distinguish execution difficulty, unavailable items, and inadequate power.
- Review an item-pool change against reward frequency, inventory pressure, crafting input supply, and content production cost.

## Evidence

- [Diablo IV Quarterly Update—December 2020](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23583664/diablo-iv-quarterly-updatedecember-2020): opening design ideals and “Skill Tree.” Full relevant article text read; pre-release intent, not shipped-rule verification.
- [Season 4: Loot Reborn](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24077223/galvanize-your-legend-in-season-4-loot-reborn): “General Item Updates,” “Tempering,” and “Masterworking.” Original article text read; historical 2024 overhaul, not a current balance reference.

Source inspection supports the observations only. The proposed checks remain unexecuted.

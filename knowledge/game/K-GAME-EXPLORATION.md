# Curiosity and usable discoveries

## Guidance

**Source-supported:** Mobius describes Outer Wilds through mysteries, locations that change over time, environmental hazards, and tools for probing, tracking signals, translating, and traversal [1]. Kelsey Beachum's official GDC abstract states the design goal of player-determined, curiosity-driven exploration rather than assigned missions, with a nonlinear story in a changing time-loop world [2]. The abstract establishes that goal and topic; it does not expose the talk's detailed techniques or their results.

**Reusable planning synthesis:** Start with questions a player could actually form after seeing or hearing something. For each discovery, record its location, prerequisites, what the player can observe, what interpretation it permits, and the next investigation it makes attractive or possible. Draw an information graph alongside the spatial map: nearby rooms need not contain consecutive knowledge, and distant clues may explain each other.

Plan several plausible leads around a central mystery, with enough partial answers that investigation produces progress before the final explanation. Decide which links require explicit naming, a recognizable landmark, a signal, or a remembered relationship. Preserve player choice by allowing competing questions; keep a recovery path for someone who misses a lead. A journal can retain observations without automatically solving their interpretation.

Prototype a compact cluster with two leads, a meaningful discovery, and a return that uses the new understanding. If locations change with time, test the information needed to predict access, the cost of waiting or retrying, and what persists after failure. Environmental danger can make observation costly; the resulting pacing should still leave room to inspect and reason. New lore, new scenery, and a discovery that changes action are different content rewards and should be budgeted separately.

## Applicability and exceptions

Useful for authored mystery exploration where information carries value. Collection-heavy open worlds, survival sandboxes, and tourism experiences may need other motivators. Knowledge cannot be forgotten on a new save, which affects replay and spoiler sensitivity. Do not equate knowledge progression with acquiring a movement ability; use the separate metroidvania entry for that topology problem.

## Checks

Proposed checks: ask first-time players what they are investigating and why; trace each inference to encountered evidence; record stalled leads, unintended spoilers, revisits, and recovery. Observe alternative discovery orders and a return after a break. Check timed access with late arrivals. These are proposed local checks, not reported Outer Wilds experiments.

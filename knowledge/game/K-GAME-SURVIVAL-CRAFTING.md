# Tie survival pressure and crafting to self-directed exploration

## Guidance

**Source observations.** Klei reports that explicit quests displaced experimentation in an early Don't Starve prototype; recipe presentation and repeated playtests replaced the checklist. Subnautica's early seabases connected exploration outposts with resource costs, power, depth, and hull integrity. Unknown Worlds later acknowledged that its nonlethal survival direction requires readable, reliable mitigation.

**Proposed reusable method.** Map an expedition as notice need or curiosity → prepare → travel and gather → face pressure → return or risk extension → craft capability → choose a new destination. Give each survival meter a purpose in that loop. State which decision it creates, how players replenish it, and whether replenishment remains interesting after mastery. A timer that repeatedly interrupts discovery without creating route or loadout choices is a candidate for revision.

Make a tool-and-resource dependency graph. Tie important upgrades to new access, safer travel, information, or different interactions. Check that the material needed to escape a region's pressure is reachable with earlier capabilities. For shelter, connect site choice to travel, supply, exposure, upkeep, and recovery.

Prototype one complete expedition with temporary presentation. Observe whether players form a second goal without a new explicit quest. Budget biome readability, recipes, creature reactions, and navigation cues together; each new area needs both attraction and a comprehensible way to survive its demands.

## Applicability and exceptions

Applies when exploration and player ownership are central. Combat-dominant survival, PvP raiding, multiplayer griefing, and long-term server economies need additional evidence. Klei's quest problem is a specific prototype result, not proof that tutorials or quests are harmful everywhere. Structured objectives can support other experience goals. The 2015 seabase post is historical Early Access behavior. The 2026 letter acknowledges problems and future work; it does not verify that later patches fixed them.

## Checks

- Ask uncoached testers to explain a need, a feasible recipe, and a self-chosen next expedition.
- Trace death or retreat to visible warning, available mitigation, execution, and recoverable loss; distinguish tension from unclear failure.
- Test the shortest valid escape and recovery routes for each resource gate, including lost equipment.
- Track repeated gathering, preparation time, travel, discovery, and abandonment; inspect whether upgrades expand decisions or merely postpone the same chores.

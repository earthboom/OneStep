# Information needs and application judgment

Use this shared procedure to decide what information a task needs and whether retrieved material can govern an action. Scout, knowledge, operations and specialists share the existing brief; do not create a second workflow, universal checklist, or usage ledger. The [knowledge procedure](../../vegabond-knowledge/SKILL.md) owns retrieval/storage, and its [retrieval reference](../../vegabond-knowledge/references/retrieval.md) owns search mechanics.

## Derive questions from the work

Start from the requested result and actual supplied artifact, not only its keywords or broad domain label. Ask which missing or mistaken fact could change the action, violate a preservation condition, or invalidate its acceptance check. Use relevant lenses:

- Intended outcome, user/reader, explicit constraints and decisions already made.
- Actual inputs, state, callers/consumers, ownership, time or revision, and dependencies across boundaries.
- Plausible failure, conflicting evidence, and what the final check would need to observe.

These are prompts, not fields that every task must fill. Inspect the nearest authoritative inputs before assuming the request describes every consequential dependency. A local spelling repair need not retrieve an entire story bible; a general fantasy chapter can still need disclosure and fair-clue reasoning. Names of domains, technologies or genres do not determine relevance by themselves.

In the existing brief, connect consequential needs as **decision/action → question and why it matters → governing source/context → evidence state → affected check or consequence**. An inline sentence is enough for small work; use a short table only when several decisions would be hard to follow. Do not invent facts to complete the table.

Distinguish information essential before a dependent action, useful for the current choice, and deferred or out of scope. Essential means its absence could invalidate the required contract, preserved meaning/canon or ability to commit safely. A preference or optional improvement is not automatically essential. Defer a question only with its boundary or reconsideration trigger; do not defer a required prerequisite merely because it is hard to obtain.

## Refine with the executing specialist

Scout forms an initial set of questions and retrieves useful starting context. After inspecting the actual code/callers, manuscript, plan, slides or other source, the specialist checks whether those questions cover the important decisions and dependencies. Add a newly exposed need; reuse already valid answers instead of restarting retrieval. Direct specialist invocations use the same check without requiring a separate scout conversation.

Examples of selective refinement, not mandatory per-domain inventories:

| Work | Questions exposed by the actual artifact |
| --- | --- |
| Simplify a callback helper | What can outlive what, on which thread, and during shutdown? Which existing behavior must survive? |
| Edit a testimony/reveal scene | What does each participant and reader know at this scene, from which observation, and what must remain undisclosed? |
| Change combat duration/reward | What happens to reward rate, progression, sinks, session experience and content demand under the stated assumptions? |
| Revise a comparison chart | Do source periods, populations, denominators and units permit the comparison? What final format was actually requested? |

Before relying on a consequential choice, challenge the initial question set: **what omitted condition would make this action wrong, and what source or check could reveal it?** Follow a concrete dependency or counterexample, not every imaginable risk. Repeat affected parts when new evidence, a changed requirement or an invalidated assumption exposes another dependency; keep valid work intact.

## Request and retrieve evidence

Pass the working project, actual decision/questions, relevance, applicable context, required evidence and known gaps to knowledge. Search knowledge and problem records, including planned actions/triggers before a symptom exists. A missing catalog match can require original-source inspection or focused research; repeated searching is not a substitute for identifying the missing question.

Knowledge returns candidates with original locators, conditions, conflicts, evidence/prevention states and gaps. It may expose a missing premise or prerequisite. The consuming specialist decides applicability and verifies the result; neither the search score nor the knowledge summary certifies whole-task sufficiency. Missing context stays unknown, and related/derived entries do not become independent corroboration.

Keep original access, content identity and claim verification separate. An entry's date/hash is not a fresh original-source check. For registered sources, reconcile every requested ID with a returned outcome, including unavailable or invalid IDs. Before asking for an unconfigured project root, reuse an already authorized path in the task or known project metadata; do not guess or search unrelated directories. Use a temporary root for this lookup unless persistent configuration was requested. Accessible unversioned originals may support a current reading, but cannot certify a historical revision; preserve that limit in the brief and final verification.

## Decide what may govern the action

For material candidates, compare the actual task with the source's project/work, version/revision/time, actors, mechanism, constraints and demonstrated evidence. Inspect the relevant original where the claim requires it. Apply the owning source's authority for the particular question: current user direction, project contracts/canon, factual evidence, preferences and provisional proposals have different roles. Recency, number of agreeing summaries or an `active` label does not settle authority.

Record the useful conclusion in the existing brief:

- **Adopt:** the source supports this action in the established context; name the resulting action/check.
- **Conditional:** specify the unresolved condition and permissible provisional use; do not silently promote it to fact.
- **Reject for this action:** identify the scope, mechanism, authority or evidence mismatch.
- **Defer:** the answer is unnecessary for the present boundary; state when it becomes relevant if material.

These decisions concern consequential use, not a log of every search hit. In particular, matching symptoms do not establish a matching cause; another author's choice does not become a universal style rule; a proposed safeguard does not become verified; a historical or future story fact does not become current character knowledge. Trace the actual mechanism and conditions before transferring a lesson.

Separate known facts, supported inferences, assumptions, conflicts and missing evidence. If an essential answer is unavailable, name the dependent action that cannot yet be justified and the smallest evidence needed; continue independent inspection or an explicitly bounded proposal. Clarify material intent with the user when needed, but do not request routine approval or block unrelated local work because the store is empty.

## Close against the work, not the search

Before handoff, connect the material decision to its supporting evidence, applied action and actual acceptance result. Check whether the artifact introduced another relevant state, dependency or factual claim. Close questions as answered, conditional, conflicting or missing; a valid deferral is not evidence that the deferred question was answered. Preserve unresolved limits and invalidated evidence with their consequences. Search coverage counts matched terms, not completeness of the task's information needs.

For skill maintenance, use the [request-to-application evaluation cases](../../vegabond-knowledge/tests/information-needs/evaluation.md). Evaluate omitted questions, wrong transfers, unsupported certainty, and unnecessary retrieval separately from catalog integrity and query-to-ID tests. No finite case set proves the absence of all future omissions.

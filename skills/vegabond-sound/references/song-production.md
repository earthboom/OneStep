# Song production

Define the substyle, intended vocal range and language, emotional trajectory, degree of roughness, and delivery. Translate reference tracks into attributes the new song can use, such as the density change into a hook, the relationship between bass and snare, or the dry-to-wide vocal contrast. Start from an original hook or beat and check its strongest and weakest phrase before arranging the full track.

## Make an initial musical choice, then compare

| Direction | Useful starting pass | What to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Korean pop ballad | Sing the core lyric over a sparse instrument and map where the vocal register and emotional intensity should peak. Plan the path from verse to chorus using density, voicing, rhythm, or range. Add piano, guitar, strings, or another color to serve that path. | At matched level, check if the chorus lift survives with less orchestration. Review lyric stress, breath points, and the last note after the climax. A key change or high belt is optional. |
| R&B or neo-soul | Pick a substyle and begin with a drum/bass pocket and a small harmonic bed. Use chord extensions or inversions where they support the lead. Compare a close lead with selective answers, doubles, and harmonies. | Compare straight and intentionally shifted parts while keeping an audible metric anchor. Assess whether bass lengths leave space for kick, whether chords crowd the lead, and where a harmony adds emotion. |
| Hip-hop | Pick a substyle, such as boom bap, trap, or sparse spoken delivery. Decide the kick and bass roles and write or mark stressed syllables, rhyme, breaths, and phrase endings against the beat. Build hook contrast through flow, texture, density, or voicing. | Check the verse over a stripped drum loop and the full beat. Hear intentional push, layback, and accent conflicts before changing timing. If the 808 serves as pitched bass, check tuning, sustain, and small-speaker translation. |

These are production starting points. For a different genre, derive the decisions from the user's references and the requested musical function. See the [ballad studies, groove experiments, and practitioner accounts](sources.md#songwriting-groove-and-arrangement) for the basis and limits of these choices.

## Develop and finish the arrangement

1. Make a lean sketch with a singable or rappable lead. Keep a tempo/key or tempo map and labeled sections. Check whether the hook is identifiable and whether the intended performer can sustain the hardest phrase in context. Verify any required section lengths against the tempo grid of the final render.
2. Build sectional contrast by choosing what enters, drops, changes register, or changes groove. A loudness-matched comparison helps separate increased level from arrangement impact. For groove edits compare a grid version with shifts on selected parts. Shape timing and velocity independently and keep intentional relationship patterns across repeats.
3. Comp the lead for continuity of tone and intention, then adjust pitch or timing where it solves an audible problem. Treat breaths, doubles, stacks, ad-libs, and effects as choices at section level. When exact words are requested, compare the sung or rapped output with the approved lyric.
4. Balance lead, rhythm, harmony, and bass on headphones and on a small or mono playback path where available. Check low-end buildup and whether hooks and consonants survive the dense sections. When stems are requested, render from the same zero point through the planned tail.

For mix-reconstructing stems, identify the reference render, allowed summing procedure, and tolerance or bit-exact requirement. Print a linear set with common sample rate, channel layout, start and end frames, and shared effect returns included once. Preserve mix gains and pan. Decode and sum at unity, then measure the difference against the reference with matched alignment. If a limiter or other nonlinear master processor is part of the reference, use an agreed pre-master reference or apply the master chain once to the recombined mix if the contract allows it. Verify the actual residual after export, including dither or quantization effects where relevant.

If using a text-to-music generator, specify original lyrics or instrumental intent, section-level changes, vocal texture and delivery, and essential timing or ending. Inspect the actual result for words, form, unwanted instruments or vocalizations, and whether its deliverable really includes usable isolated and aligned stems. Use a DAW edit or rebuild where exact bar lengths, instrumental takes, or itemized stem mixes are required.

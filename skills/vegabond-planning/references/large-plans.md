# Large plans: decomposition, integration, and change management

Use this when linked subplans share goals, scarce resources, dependencies, or decisions whose changes affect other parts. Reuse the [planning method](../SKILL.md), [review checks](review.md), and [scout's scale and stage choices](../../vegabond-scout/SKILL.md#frame-and-classify). This reference coordinates planning content; the calling operation owns execution. Document count alone does not justify more stages, new tools, delegation, or a large template. A local change follows only its actual dependencies.

## Establish the whole and its sources

State the intended experience or outcome, beneficiaries, boundaries, priorities, shared constraints, and whole-plan acceptance conditions in the existing brief. Identify what is required, proposed, deferred, or still unknown. Distinguish decisions needed now from details that can wait for evidence. A complete outline does not make all its choices settled.

Locate the authoritative source for each consequential decision and its current revision or status. Reuse existing design documents, trackers, and decision history. Give linked decisions stable references when needed, with their rationale, source, and accountable owner or proposed owner. Summaries link to those sources rather than becoming competing copies. Neither document recency nor an AI-generated summary overrides established decision authority.

## Decompose by decisions and dependencies

Connect whole-plan goals to capabilities or systems, then to the rules, content, delivery units, or experiments needed for the next decision. Choose boundaries that permit meaningful checks while making shared responsibilities visible. Do not force every project into the same hierarchy or fully specify low-confidence downstream detail.

For each consequential subplan, make these relationships inspectable in prose, a small table, or existing links:

- Its parent goal, intended contribution, scope, and authoritative source.
- Inputs, outputs, shared rules/resources, and the other plans that supply or consume them.
- Confirmed or proposed responsibility, critical assumptions, unresolved choices, and relevant acceptance evidence.

Look for missing contributions to a top-level goal, duplicated ownership or cost, and dependencies with no provider. If plans depend on each other circularly, identify the shared decision or bounded experiment that can resolve the cycle; separate document-writing assignments alone do not resolve it.

## Sequence refinement and verification

When a checked intermediate result is needed before dependent work, have scout retain or choose `staged`. Name the result, its check, and the downstream decisions it enables. Possible boundaries include direction, a critical mechanism, a representative integrated slice, or delivery readiness; select actual needs rather than imposing all of them.

Refine uncertain choices through proportionate research or authorized prototypes before committing dependent detail. Return findings to the affected plan and update the brief. Keep unblocked work moving while identifying which decisions remain conditional. An internal verification checkpoint does not create a routine user-approval requirement. Respect existing authorization and seek human decisions only where material intent or authority remains unresolved.

## Integrate across boundaries

Review both each subplan and their combined behavior. Trace a representative end-to-end user or operational sequence across the relevant plans: what each part receives, changes, and passes on, including important failures or recovery paths. Reconcile shared terms, quantities, units, timing, ownership, and assumptions at those boundaries.

Check total demand against shared capacity and budgets, avoiding double-counted resources and unowned integration work. Examine feedback loops and conflicting local targets: optimizing one part can defeat the whole-plan goal. Recompute affected totals and challenge a plausible adverse case. Do not declare integration complete merely because every document passes its local checklist.

Use the available domain methods for detailed design and real-world checks. For a game, connect intended player experience to the core loop, interacting systems, content needs, and production limits; use relevant design methods and playtesting when required. A coherent document or spreadsheet does not establish fun, balance, retention, or implementation readiness. Planning-only authorization does not authorize building or running a proposed test.

## Control a cross-plan change

1. **Identify the change.** Link the proposed decision, reason or new evidence, current authoritative revision, and requirements that must remain intact. Distinguish a correction from a changed goal or preference.
2. **Trace impact.** Follow upstream assumptions and downstream consumers, including cross-links through shared rules, resources, measures, milestones, and checks. State affected sources and consequences, unaffected boundaries, and unresolved reach. A list of edited files is not an impact analysis.
3. **Resolve the decision.** Apply the existing scope and authority. Compare relevant options, including retaining the baseline when viable. Expose conflicts with established constraints; do not silently relax them or treat a proposal as accepted. Settle routine authorized choices without adding approval gates.
4. **Propagate coherently.** After resolution within authority, update the governing source and affected summaries, subplans, estimates, and validation plans. Preserve the superseded rationale and revision history through the existing system. Mark unresolved dependents and evidence invalidated by the change as pending recheck; do not relabel historical results as if they tested the new revision.
5. **Recheck and close.** Repeat affected local and integration checks. State the new baseline, actual evidence, remaining conditions, and next decision. Retain valid work and expand the review only if the impact reaches further; partial propagation is not a fully integrated change.

For example, shortening a game's combat while keeping rewards can change rewards per unit time, progression, resource sinks, session structure, and required content throughput. Trace those consumers and the top-level experience before deciding which values to change. A calculated reward rate supports the impact analysis, not a claim that the revised game is balanced.

## Hand back a maintainable plan

Lead with the whole-plan direction, important tradeoffs, supported scope, and next decision. Link the current sources, dependency relationships, and affected decision history where useful. Show which parts are defined, provisional, awaiting checks, or blocked, and which integrated scenarios were actually reviewed. Do not equate a pile of completed subplans with verified whole-plan coverage, or a document handoff with successful execution. Keep remaining questions with the existing plan rather than creating a parallel status system.

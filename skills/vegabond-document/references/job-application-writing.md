# Job application writing

## Read the posting and select evidence

- Capture the role and level, tasks, required versus preferred qualifications, each prompt and its subquestions, and restrictions from the current posting, attachments, and portal. For a named employer, use its current job page, team or product material, or public reports for facts used in the application. Flag consequential disagreements between sources. When no target is supplied, prepare reusable material or a role-specific draft at the specificity the inputs support.
- For a package, build a small working map with **requirement or prompt | applicant evidence | ownership and scope | supported result | use or gap**. For a short edit, make the same distinctions in the working context. A requirement can be demonstrated, transferable, or unestablished. Preserve the actual title, period, status, and level of use when transferring experience across roles or languages.
- For an important achievement, establish what changed, the applicant's action, collaborators, and how the result is known. For a metric, check the baseline, endpoint, unit, denominator and period as applicable. Distinguish percentage change from percentage-point change, measured outcomes from estimates, input scale from impact, and team outcomes from personal ownership. Qualify defensible estimates when permitted and when the basis is known. When an outcome is unverified or confidential, use a supported deliverable, scale, constraint, or observable result you can disclose.
- If more input would change the result, ask for the smallest missing piece, such as the applicant's decision in a project, the definition of a metric, or the reason this particular opening matters to them. With sparse material or a short deadline, deliver the useful portion now and note what still needs confirmation. Preserve confidential client information and personal details according to the user's disclosure scope and submission instructions. Keep internal notes and source pointers out of submission text unless required.

A foreign-language equivalent may need a short description if the original credential or title does not map cleanly.

## Check the submission

- **Accuracy and relevance.** Trace key claims in the final text to an applicant statement, supplied records or a verified employer source. Check that every prompt is answered and that each important qualification is correctly represented. Read the draft as a reviewer and ask which action, result and evidence remain visible. Check the applicant can explain the claim in an interview. Remove empty praise, template filler, speculative motives and keywords unsupported by the record.
- **Form and blind review.** Respect the posting and template, including which fields may contain identifying details. Check indirect signals such as a project name, school email domain or portfolio URL. Inspect visible attachments, image and hyperlink content, filenames, and identifiable document properties where relevant. Retain identifiers in administrative fields where the form calls for them. For an AI-use restriction, follow its stated scope and offer only permitted help.
- **Length and transfer.** Recount the exact text after the final edit. Account for spaces, line breaks, word limits, encoding, or byte rules as specified. Use a local count whose unit you can state and compare with the portal preview when accessible. A local count is provisional when the platform's convention is unknown. Check headings, Markdown, links and line breaks in the actual channel where they will appear.
- **File and parsing.** Use the employer's template, file type, size limits and filename rules. For unrestricted, machine-parsed résumés, a simple single-column text layout with standard section names is a practical starting point. Keep essential text in the document body and selectable. Inspect the rendered pages and extracted reading order, checking that section headings, dates, bullets, links and Korean glyphs survive export. Check auto-filled fields when the authorized preview is available. Local extraction can detect problems. Platform parsing and screening decisions require their own evidence.
- **Delivery status.** Keep reviewer notes separate from the applicant's copy. State unresolved facts or constraints that would prevent it from being ready to submit. A review request calls for findings and targeted examples within the requested scope. An editing request calls for the revised result.

## Example from supplied facts

A five-person customer support team piloted a revised routing checklist for one week. The applicant drafted the checklist and tracked incomplete handoffs. There were 12 incomplete handoffs out of 60 in the preceding week and 6 out of 60 during the pilot. The role asks for process documentation. The applicant has confirmed an interest in helping new staff learn procedures.

- **Résumé.** “Drafted a support handoff checklist and tracked incomplete handoffs during a five-person team pilot.”
- **Experience statement.** “I drafted the checklist and logged incomplete handoffs during the team's one-week pilot. The team recorded 6 incomplete handoffs out of 60 during the pilot, compared with 12 out of 60 the week before.” The observed change is from 20% to 10%, a decrease of 10 percentage points. Keep the comparison scoped to those weeks. The records establish the difference, and the pilot by itself leaves the cause uncertain.
- **Motivation.** “I want to help new staff learn support procedures. Having drafted a handoff checklist and tracked missed handoffs during a team pilot, I can bring that approach to the process documentation this role calls for.” An organization-specific reason would need its own evidence.

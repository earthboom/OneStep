# Vegabond Fiction Design

Choose narrative structure and intended effects for the calling operation or [vegabond-fiction](../vegabond-fiction/SKILL.md). Reuse its brief, relevant sources and voice baseline. Follow the [agent instructions](../../agents/vegabond-agent.md); design neither starts another workflow nor establishes reader consensus. If invoked directly, fill missing context through [scout's shared setup](../vegabond-scout/SKILL.md#frame-and-classify) and [fiction's context retrieval](../vegabond-fiction/SKILL.md#gather-the-relevant-story-context).

Keep canon, author decisions, provisional inventions, character beliefs and unknowns distinct. Existing sources and [knowledge](../vegabond-knowledge/SKILL.md) own context/storage; link them rather than duplicating profiles or a story bible. A design-only request produces a design, not unsolicited manuscript revisions. A requested exploratory sample may test a direction, but its inventions and observed effects do not become canon or audience evidence automatically.

## Shape the narrative

1. Ground the scope (work, arc or scene), intended audience/genre/effect and preservation conditions in the existing brief. Identify the choice to settle and any material missing context; clarify unresolved intent only when it changes the work. Reuse valid design decisions and context.
2. Consider the twenty elements below at whole-work scope. For an arc/scene/local revision, select those that affect its function and actual dependencies; identify a material deferred element or unavailable context without inventing an answer. Treat them as connected questions, not twenty required devices, scores or separate skills. A static character, quiet scene, coincidence, ambiguity or open ending can be intentional; justify it against the work rather than forcing change, conflict or closure everywhere.
3. Connect relevant choices: purpose -> character desire/information/pressure -> choice/event -> consequence or change -> information available to viewpoint character and reader. Arrange scenes, revelations and setups/payoffs accordingly; identify meaningful tradeoffs such as clarity versus suspense or pace versus emotional preparation. Compare alternatives only when they affect the decision; respect discovery writing instead of imposing a fixed plot formula.
4. Before returning a design or advancing dependent writing, read and apply [evidence-based design review](references/review.md). It owns the trace from consequential choices to setting/manuscript passages, causal and disclosure challenges, element tradeoffs, and affected revisions. The twenty element names are prompts, not proof that the design works. Use the review to repair supported contradictions and expose material gaps without inventing evidence, changing author constraints, or resolving an intended mystery prematurely.

## Twenty design elements

| Element | Meaning | Design question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plausibility | How believable events or actions are under the given circumstances. | Can the reader understand why this could happen here? |
| Verisimilitude | How real the fictional world and people feel. | Do the people and their actions feel lived-in within this world? |
| Causality | Connections between causes and consequences. | How do prior events and choices produce what follows? |
| Consistency | Continuity of established setting, character and rules. | Are changes supported rather than silently contradicting what was established? |
| Character motivation | Reasons and desires behind action. | Why does this person want this and choose this action now? |
| Character depth | Multiple facets and internal tensions beyond a functional role. | What makes this person more than a plot function? |
| Character change | Effects of experience and choices on a person. | What changes, or meaningfully resists change, in thought, attitude or relationships? |
| Conflict | Clashing aims, pressures and obstacles to a goal. | What obstructs the character and what choices does it require? |
| Tension | Interest, concern or uncertainty about the outcome. | What is at stake in success or failure, or keeps the reader awaiting an answer? |
| Composition | Arrangement of events and information. | In what order should the story be experienced for its intended effect? |
| Setup and payoff | Earlier cues acquiring later consequence or meaning. | Have important resolutions or turns been prepared appropriately? |
| Pacing and modulation | Speed, compression, expansion and alternation of pressure/rest. | Where does the story linger, rush, tighten or release, and why? |
| Viewpoint control | Whose perception governs narration and its information limits. | Is each disclosure compatible with viewpoint and narrator reliability? |
| Information delivery | Timing and amount of information needed for understanding. | Can readers follow what they need now while intended questions remain open? |
| Emotional credibility | Support for feelings and emotional transitions. | What in this person's experience and situation supports this feeling now? |
| Theme | Questions or perspectives the work explores. | What question emerges through events and choices rather than an imposed explanation? |
| Style and readability | Distinctive expression and intelligibility in the intended voice. | How will diction, rhythm and narrative distance serve the chosen effect? |
| Originality | A work's own interpretation of even familiar material. | What is distinctive in its people, perspective or combination without forced novelty? |
| Ending satisfaction | How the ending answers or deliberately reframes accumulated expectations. | Does the ending fit what the story has built, including intentional openness? |
| Resonance | Feelings or questions that remain after reading. | What should remain with the reader, and what choices could support it? |

## Return the design

Apply the [shared reader-facing design guidance](../../agents/references/design-communication.md) when composing and handing back the result. First explain what happens, why the character chooses it, and what the reader knows or awaits; then connect source/scene anchors, element reasoning, tradeoffs and manuscript checks to those choices. The reader should not need to study all twenty element analyses to understand the scene. Make provisional additions explicit and propagate changed decisions only through affected scenes, characters, timelines and disclosures.

Separate source-confirmed continuity constraints, reasoned editorial choices and intended reader effects. Design can support an effect without proving that an actual reader experiences it; proposed satisfaction, originality or resonance is not a measured outcome. Name what the writer must inspect in the manuscript and any audience evidence a required claim still lacks. A missing consequential canon fact blocks only writing dependent on it; independent design can continue. The caller drafts/revises and applies [manuscript review](../vegabond-fiction/references/review.md) to the actual text, returning here only for changed narrative choices. Do not overwrite established canon or weaken author constraints to make a design look finished.

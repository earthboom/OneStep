# Unlaned sustained-inflow experiment — 2026-11-08

User-authorized continuation of the experimental Unity lab. Preserve all previous source and evidence; create a separate StreamLab scene/build. This is a research prototype. The goal is to exercise opposing and crossing mixed-radius movement without authored lanes, track every arrival request (including off-map waiting), detect collision/global deadlock/individual starvation symptoms, and measure complete frames including online planning and spawning.

## Decisions and tradeoffs

Use a static undirected four-neighbour grid (32×24, spacing 1.5), three layouts with obstacles, eight boundary portals, radius .20/.35/.60, 0.04 clearance, speed 3. Neighbours have no lane/direction annotations and paths are computed per request. This is unlaned graph motion, not unrestricted navmesh steering. RHCR's inspected abstract supports the lifelong/time-window framing; PIBT's inspected abstract explicitly restricts its reachability guarantee to graphs with cycle conditions. Do not import that guarantee into bottlenecks or unequal discs. Pure reactive yielding is rejected as a liveness argument; implementing ORCA alone would not settle starvation. Selected: custom centralized complete-route space-time search with conservative radius-aware swept reservations and FIFO admission.

Requests enter an external queue with immutable IDs and release times. A body appears only after its entire finite route has been reserved. Its first half-second interval waits at the portal, then cardinal movements interpolate linearly. Planned paths are immutable and finish within a 96-action horizon; completed bodies leave the simulation. Failed head planning stops younger admission for that epoch; no pending request is dropped/re-aged. Unreachable goals are explicitly rejected and counted. Max active bodies 256. Pending counts, ages and all job dispositions stay visible; off-map requests are not claimed as physical crowds.

A bounded time-expanded search considers waits and all legal directions; existing finite reservations expire. If FIFO head is blocked, no younger plans are added, so existing plans drain and leave a static reachable route. This conditional argument requires horizon >= static shortest route plus initial wait, finite older requests, advancing simulation, a functioning exhaustive finite search and no external geometry changes. It is not a proof for arbitrary continuously present crowds. Overload can grow queue length/latency without an individual being permanently bypassed. Finite runs detect deadline/stall symptoms and test bounded completion; they cannot experimentally observe infinity.

## Checks and phases

First capture meaningful engine red checks for radius-aware swept conflict, terminal segment sensitivity, search-around-obstacle, bounded FIFO admission, explicit unreachable disposition and deliberate starvation sensitivity. Then exercise full rendered behavior with balanced, biased, bottleneck, overload and sustained seed variants, stop inflow and drain every valid ID. Track admissions and completions during inflow, consecutive active immobility, request-to-spawn/completion, unresolved oldest age, per-direction service and queue samples. Positive fixtures require no pair/wall penetration, no teleports, no global active stall >5 s, no pending/active job beyond a declared 240 s response deadline, and complete drain by 600 s after inflow stops. Deadline failures remain findings; do not erase them with averages.

Audit every complete half-second linear action independently using doubles and each radius, including arrival segments and births at portal positions. Native Unity wall overlaps read the actual pooled transforms/scales at fractions 0/.25/.5/.75/1 of each action, with a separate displayed-to-model mapping check. A LIFO control continually lets fresh jobs bypass one old request while completions continue; it must trigger the per-ID age check. A reservation-disabled control must expose actual overlap. A stationary-body unit control must trigger global stall detection. Controls are expected adverse evidence, not safe modes.

The choke-overload fixture is explicitly adverse capacity evidence: its 240 s response criterion fails in the pilot (61 late IDs, maximum 280 s), although every valid ID eventually drains. Preserve the failed response criterion as `deadlineMet=false` and classify the scenario expectation as detecting overload, never as a positive latency pass. Seven ordinary sustained fixtures require that deadline as well as collision-free drain; the eighth drained stream is this overload comparison. Pending bodies are outside the world and included in per-ID response accounting.

After checks, run final Editor PlayMode, release build, native controls, and three sequential visible release players. Real-time 60Hz updates interpolate .5 s actions; online planning, births, completions and native transform sync remain timed. Prefill and one-second warmup are outside six-second capture windows. Measure low/medium/high arrival rates in both baffles and choke layouts, with actual active/visible population distributions, all-frame and planning-bearing following wall distributions, p95/p99/max/misses, backlog, GC, engine CPU/render/GPU coverage and source/assembly identities. A 16.67ms p95 result is not a claim that planning spikes meet the deadline. Profile/route planning is included, unlike TrafficLab's setup-only fields. Two initial baffles-only runs are preserved as pilots; native UI exposed higher choke planning cost, so final coverage adds that bottleneck rather than extrapolating the easier scene.

# Sprite, animation, and stage dialogue production in 2D games

2026-11-23 · Image sources, animation authoring, dialogue and localization, and scene direction in Unity and Unreal

**Specify how artwork is revised, which rules animate a character, what conditions select a line of dialogue, and how the scene is presented.** For example, after redrawing a guard's expression, regenerate the asset from that artwork. Translating a line replaces the text linked to its line ID. Stage and quest state determine when the guard speaks and what they say. These boundaries let a new stage reuse existing characters and movements while giving it its own dialogue and framing. [Stage production framework](2026-11-22-stage-content-framework.md) · [Game data management methods](2026-11-22-game-data-management-methods.md)

This report derives selection criteria and validation methods from papers, official documentation, developer talks, and production accounts. It sets out authoring paths, state ownership, and play checks for starting a project or adding content.

## 1. Choose an animation method by its authoring cost

| Method | Suitable content and authoring sources | Costs and visual differences to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hand-drawn frame animation** | Pixel art, strong poses, and changing silhouettes in fighters and platformers. The source is an Aseprite file or layered artwork with a timeline. | Artists can change shapes freely. More directions, outfits, and characters increase the amount to redraw. Distinguish adding a frame from holding a particular pose longer. [Aseprite sprite sheets](https://aseprite.org/docs/sprite-sheet/) · [Cuphead production talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024315/Animation-Bootcamp-Cuphead-Process-and) |
| **Part-based and 2D skeletal animation** | Characters that share talking, idle, and walking motions across different outfits and equipment. Sources include layered images, bones, weights, slots, and keyframes. | Shared motions and skins are reusable. Major silhouette changes, or a hand and weapon crossing, require authored deformation, drawing order, and occlusion. Check that diagonally rotated pixel art preserves the intended pixel shapes. [Spine keys and timelines](https://us.esotericsoftware.com/spine-keys) · [Unity PSD Importer](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.2d.psdimporter%4010.0/manual/index.html) |
| **Author in 3D and bake to 2D** | Useful when enemies and weapons share skeletons and animations while the game uses 2D frames from a fixed camera angle. The 3D models, rigs, lighting, camera, and render output specification are the sources. | Revising poses and regenerating multiple movements is easier. Output resolution, outlines, pixel alignment, and frames per direction and equipment variant determine production volume and texture costs. [Thomas Vasseur on making Dead Cells](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-) |
| **Hybrid** | Author everyday actions on a shared rig and use separate frames for silhouette-sensitive attacks, expressions, or effects. Dialogue portraits can swap only expressions or mouth shapes. | Align pivots, attachment points, start and end poses, and asset usage across output methods. Use a representative character to determine at what volume each method pays off for repeated authoring. [Skullgirls animation talk](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020575/) · [Ren’Py side images](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/side_image.html) |

Lasseter's SIGGRAPH paper explains how traditional 2D principles such as squash and stretch, anticipation, timing, and staging carry into computer animation. Apply them by asking whether a briefly shown pose reads clearly, what to emphasize, and whether players can anticipate the next moment in an action. *Skullgirls* demonstrates strong, fluid motion within a frame budget. *Cuphead* and *Dead Cells* illustrate how the desired art style and the team's cost of iteration can justify different authoring paths. [Lasseter paper](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~fussell/courses/cs384g-fall2011/projects/animator/lasseter.pdf) · [Skullgirls talk](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020575/) · [Cuphead talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024315/Animation-Bootcamp-Cuphead-Process-and) · [Dead Cells production account](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)

### The contract between frames and game state

- **Name clips for what the action means.** For example, define `guard.idle`, `guard.talk`, and `guard.turn` and their direction and skin combinations. The owner decides whether a clip loops, what state follows it, transition priority, and how it restarts. Decide whether to draw each direction or mirror horizontally. Inspect the mirrored result separately for weapon grips, lettering, and asymmetric scars.
- **Distinguish playback time from frame number.** If the Aseprite source has frame durations and tags, verify how long each pose actually appears after export and engine import. Unreal Flipbooks use FPS and the duration of each key in frames. Even with the same tags and clips, changing tools or reimporting can change hold times and loop boundaries. [Aseprite file specification](https://github.com/aseprite/aseprite/blob/main/docs/ase-file-specs.md) · [Unity Aseprite Importer features](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.2d.aseprite%403.0/manual/ImporterFeatures.html) · [Unreal Flipbooks](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/paper-2d-flipbooks-in-unreal-engine)
- **Assign ownership according to what each signal means.** Distinguish footsteps, blinks, and mouth shapes played by a clip from attacks, input, and progression evaluated by the game. If a particular pose drives gameplay evaluation, specify the connection, cancellation behavior, and how many evaluations occur when looping or changing speed. Send hit feedback for a contact outcome only after that outcome is confirmed. [Spine Events timeline](https://us.esotericsoftware.com/spine-events) · [Attack, hurtbox, and physics evaluation contract](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-HITBOX.md) · [Game feel and impact feedback research](2026-11-23-game-feel-and-impact-feedback.md)

## 2. Manage image sources, imports, atlases, and distribution

| Unit of work | Information and criteria to maintain |
| --- | --- |
| **Authoring sources** | Record the `.aseprite` and `.psb` files, rig projects, and 3D assets that artists actually edit, along with required palettes and file links. Confirm source ownership, editing rights, asset origins, and permitted uses during production. For large binaries, consider LFS or checkout according to the working environment and repository. [Aseprite CLI](https://aseprite.org/docs/cli/) · [Git LFS configuration](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/working-with-files/managing-large-files/configuring-git-large-file-storage) · [Unreal Perforce guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-perforce-as-source-control-for-unreal-engine) |
| **Import contract** | Specify image dimensions, alpha, filtering, compression, pixels per world unit, pivots, original bounds and offsets before trimming, and anchors for the ground, hands, and speech bubbles. Check pixel-art filtering and camera scale at the real output resolution and in motion. [Unity Sprite Atlas settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-SpriteAtlas.html) · [Paper 2D Sprite Editor](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/paper-2d-sprite-editor-in-unreal-engine) · [Pixel art rendering reference](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-PIXEL.md) |
| **Generated outputs** | Regenerate PNG frames, atlases, frame coordinate and duration metadata, and engine clips from source. If an imported output contains a value that requires manual editing, decide whether to put that value in a separate source. Record source locations, export settings, tool versions, and target formats. [Aseprite CLI](https://aseprite.org/docs/cli/) · [TexturePacker metadata export](https://www.codeandweb.com/texturepacker/documentation/custom-exporter) · [Assets generated by the Unity Aseprite Importer](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.2d.aseprite%403.0/manual/ImporterFeatures.html) |
| **Atlases and loaded lifetimes** | Identify images frequently drawn or loaded together and check actual batching requirements. Shared UI, the current region, portraits used in long conversations, and optional packs can have different lifetimes. Consider both how well an atlas fills its space and the cost of textures resident at the same time. [Unity Sprite Atlas settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-SpriteAtlas.html) · [Spine Texture Packer](https://us.esotericsoftware.com/spine-texture-packer) |

Repacking a derived sheet can move its rectangles. **Look up assets by keys with stable meanings in gameplay and dialogue.** Manage sheet coordinates and ordering during import. Preserving original anchors and offsets when trimming transparency helps keep the character's feet from jumping when idle changes to talking. With filtering, mipmaps, or target compression, look for bleeding from adjacent images at representative zoom levels. When adjusting padding or extruding edge pixels, also check mirroring, rotated packing, and UI masks. [Aseprite export options](https://aseprite.org/docs/cli/) · [TexturePacker atlas settings](https://www.codeandweb.com/texturepacker/documentation/texture-settings)

Atlases help batching when they fit the actual shared materials, draw order, and texture binding requirements. Rectangle packing fills space on a sheet. Actual scene and UI usage determines which images belong together. If entering a new region keeps a large sheet resident for a single portrait, compare splitting the atlas. Measure package size, on-device texture memory, initial load, and resource release after leaving a region separately from source PNG size. Also compare duplication of shared artwork across regional atlases. [Jylänki's technical report on rectangle packing](https://github.com/juj/RectangleBinPack/blob/master/RectangleBinPack.pdf) · [Unity Sprite Atlas grouping](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/unity2d/sprite/atlas/atlas-introduction) · [Game development performance and streaming research](2026-11-22-game-development-practices.md)

## 3. Define how dialogue is authored and how long line identities last

| Authoring method | When to choose it | Extra work if the fit is poor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spreadsheets and typed engine assets** | Greetings, short combat or environmental lines, fixed tutorials, and simple conversations. Authors can search or bulk-edit individual lines or connect voice and portraits in the engine. | With many conditions and branches, reconstructing a conversation from its rows becomes difficult. Preview or navigation tools become necessary to inspect choice order and conditions. [Unreal data-driven gameplay](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/data-driven-gameplay-elements-in-unreal-engine) · [Yarn Spinner localization](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/3.1/yarn-spinner-for-unity/assets-and-localization/inbuilt-localisation) |
| **Dialogue scripts and graphs** | Writers primarily edit choices, reconverging branches, speaker changes, and emotional shifts. Yarn Spinner and ink are examples. | Define the interface through which dialogue uses project flags and commands. As graphs grow, editor navigation and human-readable change comparisons become important. [Writing with ink](https://github.com/inkle/ink/blob/master/Documentation/WritingWithInk.md) · [Yarn Dialogue Runner](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/components/dialogue-runner) |
| **Line selection by conditions and priority** | Players frequently revisit stages, or NPCs choose short lines in response to world changes. Authors specify conditions, priority, repeatability, and whether to repeat unheard lines. | Selection must be traceable when several conditions match or none do. For scenes where narrative order matters, specify required prerequisites and fallback paths. [Emily Short on Storylets](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/) · [Valve talk on dynamic dialogue](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015528/Do-Say-The-Right-Thing) |
| **Script languages centered on dialogue and direction** | Games such as visual novels where speaking, backgrounds, expressions, voice, and transitions form the primary play experience. Ren’Py is an engine that expresses these tasks in one script. | If the game also combines world exploration, combat, and streaming, define how far stage state and dialogue commands need to interact. [Ren’Py dialogue and narration](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/dialogue.html) · [Ren’Py voice](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/voice.html) |

**Give each line a stable ID.** Start with a `LineId`, speaker, source text, translation context, and position in the conversation or owning node. Where used, link portrait and expression keys, voice takes, subtitle segments, and the surrounding context. Keep the ID linked to translations and recordings after revising a line, and mark source revisions that need retranslation or rerecording. When splitting a line in two or changing its meaning, decide how to handle existing recordings and saved conversation progress. [Yarn Spinner tags and metadata](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/write-yarn-scripts/advanced-scripting/tags-metadata) · [Unity Localization String Tables](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.localization%401.5/manual/StringTables.html) · [Unreal text localization](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/text-localization-in-unreal-engine)

**Assign responsibility for choices, state, and presentation assets.** The same line may be spoken in several stages, while stage and campaign policies determine eligibility and playback history. Have the relevant state owner validate and apply dialogue commands that affect play, including quest acceptance, rewards, and unlocks. If script variables and world flags would both store the same fact, define how one reads it from the other. Verify that reading dialogue in preview cannot actually grant an item and that retries or repeated execution cannot duplicate outcomes. [Running ink, state, and external functions](https://github.com/inkle/ink/blob/master/Documentation/RunningYourInk.md) · [Yarn Spinner Dialogue Runner](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/components/dialogue-runner)

## 4. Define which dialogue starts and stops in a stage

| Role | Data and responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| **Dialogue definition** | Lines, choices, branches, speakers, line IDs to display, and required conditions and commands. Minimize how much a reusable conversation needs to know about individual stages and presentation assets. |
| **Dialogue placement in a stage** | `StageId`, a stable role ID for a spawn or object, the conversation to connect, and entry conditions. Place actual triggers such as approach, interaction, region entry, or objective completion. Stage-specific camera positions and speech bubble anchors can also be connected during assembly. |
| **Dialogue session** | Owns the current line, choice, and speaker, plus temporary input and camera control. Resolves priority, interruption, replacement, and queuing when triggers compete, and determines subtitle and voice lifetimes. Cleans up running tasks and temporary controls when the player leaves the scene or a speaker disappears. |
| **Game state and saves** | Owns persistent results such as quests, rewards, repetition limits, and learned information. Define when a line counts as `seen` or `heard`. If line-level resume is supported, define supported versions and check the impact of line and branch edits. |
| **Dialogue display and direction** | Takes the current line and speaker and emotion keys and presents text, voice, portrait or sprite state, camera movement, and gestures. Reports choice availability, automatic progression, skips, and interruptions to the session. |

### Choose a conversation mode that fits the scene

- **Short reactive lines.** Keep movement and basic controls available. Apply cooldowns and limit overlapping speech from the same speaker. Set distance, position, and subtitle visibility rules for short lines that can be heard from offscreen. Give important combat warnings and essential progression instructions priority. [Valve talk on dynamic dialogue](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015528/Do-Say-The-Right-Thing) · [Firewatch development experience with interruption and resumption](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024415/Do-You-Copy-Dialog-System)
- **Player-initiated conversation.** Display an interaction prompt and give choices and the dialogue box input focus. Decide when to cancel or preserve a line or choice if the player moves away, combat begins, or interactions with several NPCs overlap. When choices communicate outcomes, give the player enough information to anticipate their differences. [Oxenfree production talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024270/Building-Game-Mechanics-to-Elevate) · [Research on meaningful choices](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12716/12716-52-16233-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- **Scripted scenes.** Use these when the scene needs to control the camera, character positions, and input transitions. Define final positions, flags, and restoration of camera and input for skipping, death, scene transitions, and replay. Account for scene length varying with localized voice duration or the user's reading speed during authoring. [Unity Timeline Signal Emitter](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.timeline%401.8/api/UnityEngine.Timeline.SignalEmitter.html) · [Unreal Sequencer Event Track](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/cinematic-event-track-in-unreal-engine)

Select priorities to suit the genre. For example, if a survival cue occurs mid-line, interrupt a short reaction. For guidance essential to completion, play it after the danger passes or leave a readable record. When instances of the same event overlap, check `are the current stage and speaker valid? → is the line still needed? → may it take input or audio from another conversation?`. When a line repeats, make its priority, validity conditions, trigger reason, and reason for completion or interruption visible to developers. [Firewatch dialogue tools](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024415/Do-You-Copy-Dialog-System) · [Designing storylet conditions and units](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)

## 5. Direct dialogue on a 2D screen

| Technique | Selection criteria and checks in play |
| --- | --- |
| **Speech bubbles above characters** | Suitable for exploration and short reactions when location should immediately identify the speaker. Check placement and speaker identification at camera edges, with two speakers close together, with speakers offscreen or streamed out, and with long translations. |
| **Fixed dialogue box and portraits** | Use for long conversations, choices, or small characters whose expressions matter. Keep speaker names and portraits visible, and establish conventions for entrances, emotional changes, and gaze direction. Check that the UI leaves available controls visible and retains input focus. [Ren’Py dialogue](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/dialogue.html) · [Ren’Py side images](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/side_image.html) |
| **In-stage camera and gestures** | Direct attention to important objects or locations through camera framing and limits, NPC and player positions, foreground and background layers, and gesture timing. In a side-scroller, verify that players can identify an exit or threat after the camera moves and that input has returned. [Itay Keren's GDC talk on side-scrolling cameras](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022244/Scroll-Back-The-Theory-and) · [GDC talk on Celeste level layout and story](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024307/The-Gamer-s-Brain-How) |
| **Text, voice, and mouth shapes** | For each mode, decide whether dialogue waits, advances on input, or advances at the end of the voice clip. When revealing text instantly, skipping voice, advancing lines, or handling missing voice, check that the portrait and mouth shapes advance to the correct line. Keep narration and offscreen speakers identifiable. [Yarn Spinner voice-over and localization sample](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/yarn-spinner-for-unity/samples/sample-guide-voice-over-and-localisation) · [Xbox subtitle and caption guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/104) |

### Review localization and accessibility in the same scene

Check long lines, Korean, Japanese, and Chinese line breaking and missing glyphs, text sizes across screens and devices, and right-to-left display where supported, all in the actual scene. Provide context for translators handling variable substitution, plurals, and name pronunciation. Decide how the game handles speaker names, sound effect captions, text contrast, ordering for overlapping lines, and access to earlier dialogue. Use pseudolocalization to catch layout problems early. Check line breaks, fonts, and recording lengths in each actual localized build. [Unicode line breaking algorithm](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr14/) · [Unity String Tables](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.localization%401.5/manual/StringTables.html) · [Xbox subtitle and caption guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/104) · [OneStep accessibility and onboarding reference](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-ACCESSIBILITY-ONBOARDING.md)

## 7. Design example for adding one stage

Add `mill-yard` to a side-scrolling adventure. Reuse the shared guard's idle, walking, and talking clips and portrait keys. The guard gives a short greeting on the first approach. Interacting presents a choice to open the mill. On a return visit after accepting the quest, the guard explains the next objective. After the objective is complete, the guard selects a completion line.

| Authoring task | What to change and which result to check |
| --- | --- |
| **Images and movement** | If the guard needs a new expression, add it to the source artwork and verify the same foot and face anchors after import. If the mill needs a dedicated gesture, add a clip and check that talking transitions back to idle. |
| **Lines, translation, and voice** | Create contextual line keys such as `mill.guard.first-greeting` and `mill.guard.open-question` and link the speaker, translation notes, and voice. After editing the main text or voice, verify that keys and order in the stage placement remain correct. |
| **Stage placement and conditions** | Assign the guard's role and location, approach and interaction triggers, and conversations for prior progression states. Keep the approach reaction from overlapping the conversation that prompts a choice. Select the correct line on revisits and in older saves. |
| **Direction and state confirmation** | Connect the short motion and portrait transition to the dialogue session's line. Evaluate the choice or game state to determine when the mill actually opens, and begin camera movement according to the confirmed state. After a skip or exit, follow the predefined policy for subsequent dialogue and control. |

Author five moments separately when directing the scene, **approach → greeting → dialogue choice → show the result → return control**. Show the approach greeting in a bubble while the player moves. Frame the guard and player together only once the player initiates conversation. Play the guard's gesture toward the mill when its line begins. Show the door moving when the choice to open it is confirmed. Restore the original camera and input when the scene ends or is skipped. Even if translation or recording length changes, each action should occur on its specified dialogue event. [Itay Keren's talk on side-scrolling cameras](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022244/Scroll-Back-The-Theory-and) · [Firewatch dialogue system](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024415/Do-You-Copy-Dialog-System)

## 8. Regression checks for imports and dialogue direction

1. **Import and distribution.** Check pivots, hold times, and clip references after adding tags or layers, reimporting, mirroring directions, or trimming. Inspect texture color, alpha, filtering, and packing at representative resolutions and on target platforms. Travel between two stages and verify that images and portraits load and are released.
2. **Dialogue and localization.** Check for duplicate line IDs, broken choices and branches, missing speakers, portraits, or voice, missing localization keys, and long strings. During play, use the real list and trigger paths to check lines without voice, speaker changes, entering and skipping choices, and dialogue on revisits started with older flags.
3. **Direction and interruption.** Trigger another event immediately after approaching, leave the scene during playback, or cancel from a choice screen. Check that audio, effects, camera control, and input focus are cleaned up. After changing a shared definition or player component, verify the same actions and reactions in existing stages.
4. **Outcomes to preserve.** Set expectations separately for edits to a line's tone or expression and edits that affect a quest.

# Research on designing impact feedback and game feel

2026-11-23 · Input response, animation, camera, visual effects, sound, and haptics

**Camera, effects, and sound create a sense of impact when they communicate different information about the same outcome.** Players should see the instant a weapon makes contact, hear the difference between materials and outcomes, and retain the view and control needed for their next action after a major impact. In this document, **impact feel** means how clearly contact and its consequences can be felt. **Game feel** means the overall experience of control, from acceptance of an input through the player's next choice. In development, consider input and movement tuning, event feedback, and design that helps players carry out their intentions together. [Pichlmair and Johansen's game feel survey](https://researcher.itu.dk/p/en/research-outputs/designing-game-feel-a-survey) · [Swink's Game Feel](https://www.routledge.com/Game-Feel/Swink/p/book/9780123743282) · [Research on impact feedback in action games](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/lin22features.pdf)

This report applies the [game development strategy](../designs/game-development-strategy.md) and [gameplay regression verification rules](2026-11-22-gameplay-regression-verification.md) to impact feedback and control feel. Read values from each example in the context of its camera, genre, and input device. The examples below are design proposals to verify in an actual project.

## 1. What the research can establish

| Source | Findings and scope of application |
| --- | --- |
| **Pichlmair and Johansen, Designing Game Feel** | The authors distinguish *juicing*, which emphasizes events with feedback, *tuning*, which adjusts motion and physics, and *streamlining*, which helps players act on their intentions. These distinctions allow separate diagnoses for late input acceptance, a weapon that feels too light, and an unclear hit result. [Authors' version](https://researcher.itu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/91131028/TG3072241.pdf) |
| **Lin et al., Impact Feedback in Action Games, 2022** | The researchers estimated game rankings from Steam reviews and compared the top eight and bottom eight across 19 features. Hitstop, consistency between sound and visuals, and camera control emerged as candidate distinguishing features. A comparison across games cannot establish the causal contribution of a single feature or settings suitable for every genre. [Paper](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/lin22features.pdf) |
| **Kao, The Effects of Juiciness in an Action RPG, 2020** | In four conditions of one action RPG with 3,018 participants, the no-feedback and extremely excessive-feedback conditions yielded lower playtime, experience, intrinsic motivation, and performance than the medium and high conditions. Several feedback elements varied together. When adding effects to a game, measure both the desired improvement and the cost to readability. [Abstract and bibliographic record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-juiciness-in-an-action-rpg/) |
| **Hicks et al., Good Game Feel, DiGRA 2018** | From a developer perspective, the authors analyzed how apparently excessive embellishments can serve as meaningful responses. Their work supports deciding an effect's meaning and context before deciding how much to use. [Paper](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/936/936/933) |
| **Nacke, Grimshaw, and Lindley, FPS sound experiment, 2010** | In a modified FPS, the study combined conditions with sound effects and music switched on or off. Effects influenced several experience questionnaire items, and the combination of music and effects affected ratings of tension and flow. Audition the same combat effect both in isolation and in the game mix. [Paper record at the authors' institution](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/more-than-a-feeling-measurement-of-sonic-user-experience-and-psyc/) |
| **Heikkilä, Danso, and Luck, game audio meta-analysis, 2026** | Of 14 papers reviewed, six entered the meta-analysis. Conditions with game audio produced higher positive experiences such as enjoyment. Excluding some conditions reduced the estimated effect. The effect of adding music to game sound had substantial uncertainty. These results concern audio experience across games. Test the contribution of an impact sound alone on a representative hit. [PLOS ONE paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0361519) |
| **Singhal and Schneider, Juicy Haptic Design, CHI 2021** | Two phone studies examined preferences and game experience with vibration alongside visuals. The authors reported that designed vibration could increase enjoyment and improve experience. Reevaluate for console controllers or extended play with the appropriate device and duration. [Authors' paper](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/bitstream/handle/10012/16894/Juicy_Haptic_Design_SIGCHI_2021__UWSpace_.pdf) |
| **Vroomen and Keetels, review of intersensory synchrony · Spjut et al., FPS input latency case study** | Stimulus and task conditions affect judgments of audiovisual synchrony. FPS aiming calls for separate measurement of actual input-to-display latency. Record relative impact-sound timing and input response time separately. Measurements from another environment cannot supply a universal tolerance. [Synchrony review](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/perception-of-intersensory-synchrony-a-tutorial-review/) · [FPS aiming experiment](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2021-05_case-study-first-person-aiming-low-latency-esports) |

## 2. Design one hit from input to recovery

| Moment | What the player needs to know | Design techniques |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Input acceptance** | Was the button press accepted? Was the next attack queued? | When an attack actually starts, show a change in hand or weapon pose or play a startup sound. If the game supports combos or buffering, define input time, the window in which an input can be consumed, and when it expires on cancel or mode change. |
| **Wind-up and movement** | From what direction, distance, and rhythm is the attack coming? | Communicate force and direction through silhouette, weight transfer, weapon trajectory, and an audio cue. If the opponent needs time to dodge or block, the wind-up must read at the appropriate camera distance. |
| **Contact and resolution** | Did the attack miss, get blocked, or hit something? What did it hit? | Combine effects at the resolved contact position and direction with outcome-specific timbres and hit poses. Give a small impact that reinforces control feel a different presentation from a large impact that changes the fight. |
| **Aftermath and recovery** | How important are the damage and hitstun? When is the next action available? | Use a brief stop, recoil, or reverberation to carry the impact, then return the body, camera, and mix to a state where the next threat is readable. Animation and rules must agree on when an attack can be canceled or another action begins. |

**Keep the weapon fixed and vary the outcome.** A swing through air can have a whoosh and trail, a blocked swing a metallic sound and rebound off the shield, and a body hit a contact effect and directional hit reaction. If a parry or guard break changes who has the initiative, distinguish it with a unique timbre, silhouette, or both. In games where super armor prevents hitstun, separate cues can communicate health damage and whether the target staggered. [Jiesang Song on making Kingdom Under Fire](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/improving-the-combat-impact-of-action-games) · [Riot on combat clarity](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/) · [Xbox on attack cues in Grounded](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)

## 3. Animation, effects, and hitstop

- **Give wind-up, contact, and recovery recognizable poses.** Weight can come from weight transfer during preparation, a change of speed on contact, and braking in the aftermath. Contrast wind-up and recovery times for heavy and fast attacks. Check the direction in which a struck body moves and whether the visible trajectory agrees with the attack's active window. [Skullgirls animation at GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020575/) · [Production experience with melee impact](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/improving-the-combat-impact-of-action-games) · [Attack, hit, and physics collision contracts](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-HITBOX.md)
- **Give each effect a meaning.** Sparks at contact can show location, flying fragments can show direction and material, and a large flash or shattering shape can mark a change of state. A swing trail describes the attacker, while a hit effect describes the point of contact. If an area telegraph disagrees with resolution or another player's effects hide the next threat, adjust priority, size, brightness, and duration. Preserve essential boundaries and outcome cues at lower particle-count quality settings. [VFX as a Game Design Language at GDC](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027899/Visual-Effects-Summit-VFX-as) · [Riot VFX production training](https://www.riotgames.com/en/artedu/visual-effects)
- **Define the scope of hitstop.** Document which elements a brief pause or slowdown on impact affects: the attacker and target, animation, or the whole world. Define how input collection, combo, invulnerability and cooldown timers, movement resolution, and the camera, UI, and audio clocks progress. Specify hitstun separately because it restricts the next action under the game rules. In Unity, setting global `timeScale` to 0 stops `FixedUpdate` and coroutines waiting on `WaitForSeconds`. Check physics resolution and recovery together. If stops accumulate during repeated hits and swallow inputs or drag out the world simulation, define a cap or merging rule. Compare light-hit, block, and heavy-hit durations against the project's control rhythm. [Sakurai's hitstop video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OdVkEOzdCPw) · [Lin et al.'s comparative study](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/lin22features.pdf) · [Unity time scale](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Time-timeScale.html) · [Simulation step knowledge](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-TIMESTEP.md)

## 4. The camera communicates both impact and combat information

| Technique | Useful conditions | Side effects to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brief directional shake** | Emphasize the direction or strength of a melee hit, landing, or explosion. Set falloff by source position, distance from the player, and attack type. | High-frequency attacks and overlapping explosions can turn into continuous shake. Set priorities and limits on combined effects. Tune amplitude, frequency, direction, and decay independently. |
| **Framing, zoom, and shifts of attention** | The point of interest moves, as in a heavy attack's wind-up, a projectile's arrival, or a major boss action. | Check aiming, the next enemy, room to dodge, and camera collisions with walls. Third-person framing that shows a nearby enemy clearly can increase offscreen danger. |
| **Camera recoil and weapon recoil** | Convey the direction and weight of a shot as it fires in an FPS or TPS. | Recoil that changes the actual relationship between the reticle and trajectory belongs to the game rules. Reducing cosmetic shake in settings must preserve hit resolution and aim assistance. |
| **Cuts, large zooms, and cinematic cameras** | Use sparingly when play itself changes, such as a skill finisher or boss phase transition. | Coordinate the return of input control with enemies resuming action, and restore the player's view of the playable space. Specify the affected cameras separately for split screen, spectators, and multiplayer. |

Eiserloh demonstrates accumulating and decaying impact within a bounded value and mapping it to shake through a nonlinear curve. Compare this approach when ordinary hits need a small change and heavy impacts a pronounced one. Vlambeer's demo adds responses one at a time to reveal the differences. The *God of War* team describes redesigning combat itself when moving to a close, player-controlled camera. First check whether the default composition shows the attacker, contact point, and next threat. [Juicing Your Cameras With Math at GDC](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023557/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Juicing) · [Eiserloh's slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Eiserloh_Squirrel_JuicingYourCameras.pdf) · [Vlambeer talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U) · [God of War combat and camera at GDC](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026423/Evolving-Combat-in-God-of) · [Camera tracking and damping knowledge](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-CAMERA.md)

## 5. Tune the attack, contact, and aftermath of a sound separately

| Sound layer | What it conveys | Authoring and mixing decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Swing or firing preparation** | Speed, direction, weapon size, and attack timing | A whoosh or attacker's exertion can begin before contact. Give important enemy telegraphs enough priority to remain audible over the player's repeated attacks and music. |
| **Contact sound** | Hit, block, deflection, struck material, and onset of impact | Check that the sound's first transient is perceived as part of the visual contact. A short attack establishes contact, while body and material layers distinguish weight and surface. Leading silence in the file and output-device latency also affect timing. |
| **Aftermath** | Weight, scale, space, and the target's state | Choose lengths for weapon resonance, debris, hit reactions, and reverberation. If tails pile up during repeated attacks or around many NPCs, consider concurrency limits, priorities, short fades, or temporarily attenuating other sounds. |
| **Player confirmation cue** | Confirms an important outcome even at a distance or offscreen | Define separate purposes for a spatial sound and a local confirmation cue. Check whether the same event sounds as if it happened twice or obscures opponents' direction or team signals. |

**Perceived force and loudness have different controls.** Compare short onsets, material layers, and decays at the same volume. Vary ordinary hits while retaining the features that make blocks, parries, and damage sound distinct. Test on small speakers with weak bass, on headphones, and with overlapping music and dialogue. The *UNDERDOGS* talk describes producing forceful combat audio on small VR speakers. The *Kingdom Under Fire* article explains how to think separately about swings, hits, and material sounds. [UNDERDOGS sound at GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035486/Visceral-Sound-on-Tiny-Speakers) · [Kingdom Under Fire production article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/improving-the-combat-impact-of-action-games) · [Unreal sound concurrency rules](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sound-concurrency-reference-guide)

For rhythm action, establish the input window, music playback clock, and range of audiovisual calibration at the outset. Schedule known beats and transitions on the audio clock. For contact sounds that become known only after a hit, observe confirmation timing and output latency. In other action games, specifying what triggers the start of each effect makes it easier to catch a sound lagging behind after an animation edit. [Hi-Fi RUSH director at GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034256/Developing-Hi-Fi-RUSH-Backwards) · [Unity PlayScheduled](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/AudioSource.PlayScheduled.html) · [Game music timing knowledge](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-TECH-MUSIC.md)

## 6. Haptics and accessibility

Vibration can reinforce force or direction through the duration, strength, channel, and spacing of short pulses. The *Returnal* team represented rain with tiny pulses, dash direction with left- or right-hand feedback, and heavy landings with a corresponding impact. If continuous vibration masks a major impact, lower the background, keep hits short, and give important states distinct patterns. Controllers differ in motors and available patterns. Play on each supported device and with keyboard and mouse. [Housemarque and PlayStation sound team's production account](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=349009) · [Comparative haptics study](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/bitstream/handle/10012/16894/Juicy_Haptic_Design_SIGCHI_2021__UWSpace_.pdf) · [Unreal device-specific Force Feedback](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/force-feedback-in-unreal-engine)

Decide how to convey important warnings and outcomes through at least two sensory channels. For example, a successful parry can combine a short sound and distinctive shape, and an offscreen threat can combine spatial audio with a directional indicator. Offer separate adjustments for shake, head bob, motion blur, and vibration strength. Check flashes through entire combat sequences with rapidly overlapping effects. Play with reduced effects to verify that resolution and information needed to play are preserved. [Xbox XAG 103 sensory channels](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103) · [XAG 110 haptics](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/110) · [XAG 117 motion](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) · [XAG 118 flash review](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118) · [OneStep accessibility and onboarding](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-ACCESSIBILITY-ONBOARDING.md)

## 7. How game feel varies by genre

| Genre | Essential condition | Feedback to compare first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fighting and melee action** | Read the opponent's threat, attack active windows, and differences among confirmed hit, block, parry, and recovery. | Compare contact poses, brief stops, direction of the opponent's reaction, and outcome-specific sounds. Check how much effects obscure in crowd fights. |
| **FPS, TPS, and cooperative action** | Feel firing immediately while maintaining aim and distinguish the server-confirmed result. | Select firing animation and sound, confirmation audio tied to accurate hit resolution, and damage direction cues. Check camera recoil against trajectory and look for duplicated feedback under latency and correction. |
| **Platformers and racing** | Feel continuity of input and transitions in physical state. | Distinguish landing, wall contact, loss of grip, and collision. At the same approach speed, check whether players recognize changes and can adjust their next jump or steering input. |
| **Rhythm action** | Reference timing for audio, visuals, and input, plus feedback on timing errors. | Compare success, early, and late cues, audio and display latency calibration, and how judgments feel after a device change. |
| **Puzzle and turn-based games** | Make input acceptance, rule outcomes, and progression steps brief and clear. | Distinguish a moving box from a blocked one, and a card effect from the end of a turn. Look for cuts or long aftereffects that delay the next input during repeated actions. |

## 8. Connecting game outcomes and presentation in Unity and Unreal

**Shared design.** Separate the event that starts an attack from input and the event that confirms contact resolution. Combat rules own the outcome, target, contact position, and direction, plus damage and hitstun where needed. Presentation selects animation, effects, sound, camera, and haptics for that same outcome. For example, two weapons using the same block resolution can share presentation by material and strength while each attack has its own authored trail and recovery time. Keep only the events and data actually needed and use the game's existing ownership and entry points. | --- | --- | --- |

Also check default aiming and tracking camera behavior. | **Effects and animation** | Use Animator and the Particle System or VFX Graph as appropriate for the project. Pass contact position, surface, and outcome to presentation. [Sound Concurrency](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sound-concurrency-reference-guide) · [MetaSounds](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/metasounds-the-next-generation-sound-sources-in-unreal-engine) · [Force Feedback](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/force-feedback-in-unreal-engine) |

**Multiplayer.** Distinguish immediate feedback for firing or starting an attack from feedback confirming damage. Where presentation is predicted, handle confirmation and cancellation, and prevent a confirmed hit on the same target of the same attack from playing twice. Check latency, packet loss, multi-hit attacks, and simultaneous hits. In Unreal projects already using GAS, Gameplay Cues suit cosmetic feedback that may be dropped. The authoritative gameplay path handles states that must agree, such as damage and hitstun. ## 9. Design playtests and regression checks

1. **Repeat several outcomes in one scene.** Set up paths for misses, blocks, hits, and heavy hits with the same weapon, enemy, material, and distance. Review footage from the attacker's controls and, where needed, from the target and spectator perspectives. Ask separately: `Did the input receive a response?`, `Can you identify the outcome?`, `How strong did the impact feel?`, and `Can you see the next action?` [Impact feedback research](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/lin22features.pdf) · [Choosing a playtest method](../../knowledge/game/K-GAME-PROTOTYPE-PLAYTEST.md)
2. **Compare presentation under the same rules.** Vary one suspected factor at a time, such as camera strength, contact sound, or hitstop. Include combined conditions for two factors where needed. Counterbalance order to reduce the influence of becoming accustomed to the first effect. Along with preference, observe accuracy distinguishing blocks from hits, success of the next input, missed enemy telegraphs, fatigue, and discomfort. [Kao's comparison of feedback intensities](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-juiciness-in-an-action-rpg/) · [Juice It or Lose It demonstration](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016789/Juice-It-or-Lose)
3. **Check latency and event order.** Record when input is accepted, rules resolve, and animation, effects, and sound are requested. When player-perceived latency is at issue, measure from input to actual display and audio output on representative devices. Inspect the first response and later resolution during rapid inputs, low frame rate, wireless audio, and target network conditions. [FPS input latency measurement study](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2021-05_case-study-first-person-aiming-low-latency-esports) · [Audiovisual synchrony review](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/perception-of-intersensory-synchrony-a-tutorial-review/)
4. **Leave a path for checking existing quality.** Verify that reducing camera shake and vibration preserves damage, hitstun, and combo resolution, that misses show no hit effect, and that multi-hit attacks and repeated retries leave no duplicate or lingering feedback. Automate checks for event direction, event counts, and rule outcomes, then review differences in sound and control feel through play on actual devices. [Regression verification and visual and performance quality](2026-11-22-gameplay-regression-verification.md#5-reproduction-visual-quality-and-performance)

The initial design questions are **Can players feel that input was accepted? Can they tell what they hit? Can they still see the next threat when a major outcome is emphasized?** Verify these on one representative attack, then decide which criteria must hold for other weapons, enemies, perspectives, and platforms.

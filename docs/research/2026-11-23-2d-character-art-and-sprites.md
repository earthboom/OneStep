# Drawing one character for illustration, gameplay and animated sprites

2026-11-23 · OneStep image skill research

The three assets share **features that identify the character across sizes and viewpoints**. Dialogue art conveys an expression at a moment in the story, the gameplay image conveys facing and action at playing distance, and animation shows how the pose changes over time. A commission for one image needs only the deliverable appropriate to its use. The production and review criteria below are project applications of published work. This study did not retrain a generator or measure output quality.

## 1. What to carry from the illustration into gameplay

**Give the design a reason for looking the way it does.** Greg Kasavin describes deriving both Demeter’s attitude and her frost powers in *Hades* from the winter that comes during the loss of her daughter. Asking whom a character is looking at and why they hold the prop that way gives a more specific direction than “a mage in cool colors.” For variations, preserve the face shape and clothing from the neutral portrait, then check that the eyebrows, mouth and head angle communicate the expression at dialogue-box size. [Supergiant developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon)

**Give appearance and scene references separate roles.** IP-Adapter combines text and reference-image features through separate attention paths. Apply that idea with the controls available: the character illustration defines identity, the game screenshot defines camera and size, and the sketch defines pose. If reproducing a face also copies the portrait’s camera angle, separate the instructions for viewpoint and appearance first. The generator need not directly support IP-Adapter. [Ye et al., IP-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06721)

**Choose features that survive at playing distance.** Valve’s guide explains silhouette and value hierarchy at the game camera. Pedro Medeiros teaches organizing forms with connected pixel clusters. Valve’s example is a top-down 3D game. Its camera and values are specific to that context, but testing identification against the actual camera and background transfers. If embroidered details become noise as a portrait shrinks, carry the hood silhouette, scarf color block and lamp position into the gameplay sprite. For hand-drawn art, inspect line weight and translucent edges at target resolution. [Valve character art guide](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935) · [Medeiros’s pixel art lessons](https://saint11.art/pixel_articles/)

## 2. Methods for producing motion and limits of the evidence

| **Animate Anyone**, CVPR 2024 | Separate components handle reference appearance, target pose and temporal information to produce character video. | Prepare appearance references, a pose sequence and a playback review. For a still-image editor, use a reference pose and key-pose sketches. Video output alone cannot establish transparent game cels or grounded feet. [Hu et al., paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Hu_Animate_Anyone_Consistent_and_Controllable_Image-to-Video_Synthesis_for_Character_Animation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf) |
| **Sprite Sheet Diffusion**, public research manuscript | The authors manually annotated examples where stylization defeated pose extraction. Character consistency scored 0.904 for the fine-tuned model and 0.903 for the comparison model. They omitted that score in another comparison because the frames barely changed. | A consistent character can still have an unreadable walk. Compare contact and passing silhouettes and watch how the cycle returns to the next stride. Replace an ill-fitting skeleton with a pose sketch for exaggerated proportions. [Hsieh et al., experiments and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03685) |
| **Sprite Sheet Generation using a Diffusion Model**, CVMP 2025 | In an experiment turning human portraits into pixel characters, generating motion at high resolution before pixelation outperformed pixelating the input first on four metrics. The authors’ data contained 845 frames across 94 motions. | If early reduction loses hands or props, establish motion at a larger size and finish on a shared grid and palette. A single portrait or short motion study can start with a reference-image edit. [Vasseur’s production account](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-) |

Replay it with the game state when adjusting holds. ## 3. One character in three uses

The example is **a lighthouse keeper with a notched hood, an amber scarf and a lamp in the left hand**. The dimensions and motions are illustrative.

| Asset | Generation direction | Acceptance or redraw decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dialogue illustration | Chest-up shot. The keeper turns toward a sound from the sea. Lamplight reaches one cheek. | With the actual dialogue box overlaid, the face and hood silhouette remain visible. Gaze and expression alone convey listening. |
| Gameplay idle | Side-scrolling camera, full body facing right, about 40 pixels tall on screen. Leave a gap between the scarf and lamp. | Identify character and direction on a bright dock and inside a dark warehouse. If lamp and body merge, fix the arm silhouette before adding detail. |
| Walk loop | From idle, establish left contact, passing, right contact and opposite passing. Add only the necessary in-betweens. The engine moves the character. | At actual travel speed, inspect the grounded foot for sliding. Preserve scarf sway and torso rise and fall. If the entire frame shakes, compare registration in original and sliced frames. Check which hand holds the lamp when facing the other way. |

## 4. Verifying generated art as a game asset

**A fixed grid and a trimmed atlas need different reconstruction data.** With uniform cells, keep a common canvas and origin and slice with the actual importer. If transparent margins were trimmed per frame, restore the original canvas and offset as well as the atlas rectangle. Aseprite’s example records `frame`, `spriteSourceSize`, `sourceSize` and `duration` separately. Godot can assemble frames from individual images or a sheet and preview them at different speeds. Inspect the complete sheet, the sliced frames and playback at actual speed. [Aseprite author’s JSON example](https://community.aseprite.org/t/how-to-process-the-generated-json-sprite-sheet/34) · [Godot 2D animation documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_sprite_animation.html)

Inspect the same landmark twice to locate a fault. If the lamp jitters when the original poses are overlaid, repair the pose or prop. If originals align but the body jumps only in sliced playback, check pivot, trim offsets and cell dimensions. For a pixel that flickers only during playback, compare its palette and value cluster with adjacent frames. To measure whether these checks prevent defects, replay two outputs with the same design and scale in the game view and record identification failures, foot sliding, clipping and the number of repaired frames.

# Character profile questions assumed a predetermined plot outcome

## Expected and observed

The author wanted character design before deciding how that person would act in a scenario. The initial line of questioning asked what was needed for established plot work. The author objected to shaping the person as a tool for the plot and asked for different questions.

## Cause and resolution

The questions were backfilled from dramatic function. The revised interview asked what the person does on an unclaimed day, with whom silence is comfortable, which requests they accept, and what they value or regret about their previous life. The answers were saved with their limits in the owning character document.

## Recheck and prevention

The resulting canon includes ordinary routines, a relationship preference and distinct reasons to decline a request or keep a commitment without requiring the planned climax. The recheck covers the revised questions and profile, not how future scenes will read. The check for a later interview is proposed.

## Evidence

- Novel_0, novel-2. The author's instruction to separate the character interview from the scenario, and the subsequent seven answers in the project discussion.
- Protagonist canon revision history 0.18.0–0.24.0, sections on food and routines, companionship, commitments and self-acceptance. Canon and the full conversation remain in the working project.

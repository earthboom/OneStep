# Reader-facing design documents

Use this when composing or handing back a code or fiction design. It governs explanation and readability; each design skill retains its own evidence and correctness review. Use the existing brief or requested artifact, without a mandatory template, extra summary file, or routine approval checkpoint.

## Lead with the decision

Identify the intended reader and what they need to judge or do. Open with a compact decision brief: the goal, chosen direction, what changes and stays intact, the main reason and cost, and unresolved conditions or decisions. A small design can do this in one paragraph. Put any limitation that changes the apparent feasibility, guarantee, canon, or scope here, not only in an appendix. Distinguish a decision the human must make from a remaining implementation or manuscript check; say what that decision affects and the supported options when relevant.

The opening should let the reader explain the choice and its consequences without first learning every API, field, theory term, or element label. Introduce necessary terms beside the behavior they explain. Reuse the reader's known vocabulary and define unfamiliar terms briefly on first use; do not add a large glossary by default.

## Reveal detail in useful order

After the opening, walk through the behavior or narrative sequence, then the structure and reasoning needed to carry it out. Group details around consequential decisions rather than enumerating every internal step or checklist item. Use a small example, diagram, or comparison only when it reduces the explanation the reader must hold in mind.

Keep evidence, important rejected alternatives, counterexamples and verification plans available below the explanation or through precise links to existing sources. Each detail should make clear which choice or condition it supports. Do not delete necessary evidence merely to meet a length target, repeat the same claim in several sections, or create parallel human/agent versions of the design's facts. Within maintained project guidance, the existing English/Korean translation boundary still applies; task artifacts follow the requested language.

Keep the brief and detailed reasoning consistent after revisions. A shorter opening must preserve material uncertainty and tradeoffs, not imply that a partial or blocked design is fully supported. Consolidate repeated explanations and retain source anchors instead of copying whole reports or setting documents.

## Inspect understanding before handoff

Read the final artifact from the intended reader's position. Can its opening explain the goal, choice, impact and important limits? Can an unfamiliar term be understood where it first matters? Is it clear why each detailed section is needed and where to inspect its evidence? Are the human's pending decisions easy to locate? Compare the brief with the detailed design so readability edits cannot silently weaken a requirement or hide a blocker.

When relevant human feedback is available, use whether the reader can restate the choice, cost and uncertainty to improve the document. Do not require a comprehension quiz or approval on every task. AI self-review or a simulated reader may reveal confusion but cannot prove that the user understood; report such checks at their actual evidence level.
